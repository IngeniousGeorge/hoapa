## 1. Make “expression” the broad starting point, and propositions a particular articulation

Saying that the context is expressive throughout is a coherent philosophical starting point. But saying that everything is a proposition is a considerably stronger—and different—claim.

In the Spinoza–Deleuze register, expression is not necessarily a statement, representation, or message addressed to someone. Substance expresses itself through attributes and modes; an existing being need not describe anything to participate in this expressive relation. Deleuze, Spinoza et le problème de l’expression.

A proposition, by contrast, belongs to an organization of claims, judgments, consequences, and justification. An organism and a proposition about that organism cannot simply be exchanged.

I would therefore formulate your starting point as:

> We consider a context through its expressions and their relations. Logical presentations articulate some of those relations as propositions, constructions, and rules of transformation.

This preserves the possibility of the stronger metaphysical reading—everything participates in expression—without making everything already documentary content.

There is a particularly useful distinction here:

- Being an expression of a context.
- Producing expressions through which that context can be understood.

The second can emerge within the first. That is almost exactly the turn your cascade needs: some beings become capable of interpreting, articulating, and communicating aspects of the context they already belong to.

Also, if the first “subset” is the whole context, it is really the starting register, not yet a subdivision. Calling it an expressive reading of context would be clearer than “full subset.”

## 2. The logical reading makes BWR legible, but does not supply it by itself

Propositions alone do not yet give borrowing, work, or replay. Their relations and operations do.

In a logical presentation, one can recognize:

- borrowing as using assumptions, resources, or previously established constructions;
- work as making a derivation or construction;
- return as articulating what has been achieved and under which conditions;
- replay as applying that achievement in another admissible situation.

That is enough to make BWR intelligible at the level of your orientation. It does not require claiming that every physical process is literally a proof, or that logical applicability automatically produces actual uptake.

Likewise, expression alone does not establish perspectivism. The further ingredient is differentiated expression: the context becomes determinate differently through different relations and organizations. Your opening can state this explicitly rather than derive it from infinity.

The two readings then cooperate without being synonyms: expression concerns how a context becomes manifest through its beings; logic concerns disciplined articulation and transformation.

## 3. Replace “real/abstract” with “concrete existence/ideal structure”

That would be my preferred wording for this particular picture. “Real/abstract” misleadingly suggests that mathematics or general forms are less real.

You already have an anchor for ideal structure in Lautman. I would not add another philosopher merely to fill that branch. His problematic relations, mathematical realizations, and relation to physical theories give you ways of discussing different forms of determination—not a simple division between reality and unreality.

But these should be related aspects or registers, rather than entirely separate populations. A physical configuration can realize a mathematical structure; a written proof is a concrete occurrence carrying an ideal construction. The relation between the two is part of what needs explaining.

Peirce offers a useful, more specific companion: repeatable forms and their particular instances. His type/token distinction separates a significant form from an occurrence embodying it. That is especially pertinent once the cascade reaches documentation and reuse. It is not a complete ontology of physical versus mathematical being. I would therefore use:

> Concrete processes and beings; ideal structures through which their relations can be articulated.

And keep Lautman’s movement as a relation among problematics, mathematics, and physical theorization—not as a chronological manufacturing chain through which metaphysics produces matter.

Manin and Marcolli fit as a more specialized mathematical resource. Can we now rethink the relation between a) the "covariant" pre-historical & "contravariant" pre-philosophical, and b) the series of separations and there echoing integrations ?

Yes. I think we can now relate them more clearly: the two movements describe where the process goes; separation and integration describe how it proceeds without destroying what makes it possible. They are two intersecting distinctions, rather than two names for the same distinction.

## 1. The downward movement produces beings capable of a return movement

Your “covariant” pre-history follows how a context supports determinate organizations, some of which sustain themselves, interpret their surroundings, and communicate. Your “contravariant” pre-philosophy follows how those organizations articulate the conditions, relations, and problems through which they exist.

The important connection is:

> A context supports beings capable of articulating it; their articulations can become conditions for further beings and activities.

The return is therefore not simply tracing the outward route backwards. A community does not reconstruct the entire genesis of its existence. It produces a particular understanding, with particular powers of explanation and use. That understanding can introduce distinctions and possibilities that were not available to the community before.

This also lets us distinguish two achievements: understanding an enabling context and producing something that becomes enabling context. They can coincide, but neither guarantees the other.

## 2. Separation and integration operate in both directions

The tempting assignment would be “pre-history separates; pre-philosophy integrates.” It catches something, but misses half the process.

| Movement | What separation contributes | What integration contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Context → situated beings and activities | Distinct organizations, capacities, interfaces, and ways of participating can arise. | Relations with a milieu make those distinctions viable and effective. |
| Situated activity → articulated context | Inquiry distinguishes concepts, dependencies, assumptions, and differences between perspectives. | Those distinctions become connected explanations, reusable constructions, and conditions for further inquiry. |

An organism, for example, is not produced by separation alone: its relative independence depends on organized relations within itself and with its milieu. Conversely, understanding is not integration alone: a good explanation often succeeds by discovering a distinction that an earlier account obscured.

So the stronger formulation is:

> The outward movement makes differentiated participation possible; the return movement makes the relations of that participation articulable. Each requires both separation and integration.

This is where your mirror becomes reciprocal rather than merely symmetrical. What enables a distinction in one setting can become something explicitly distinguished in another; what has been distinguished can then support a new integration.

## 3. The mirror connects practical separations with their enabling conditions

This gives us a useful interpretation of the K/M investigation.

On K, we encounter a practical requirement: two responsibilities must not be confused. On the contextual side, we investigate the relations that allow them to remain distinct while working together.

For example:

- Participants must remain distinct from their documentary contributions. The corresponding integration question is how contributions can form shared understanding without requiring exhaustive representations of their contributors.
- Local work must remain situated. The integration question is under what compatibility conditions its results can contribute to something beyond their original situation.
- Stabilized concepts must remain distinguishable from their possibilities of variation. The integration question is how reusable forms can participate in families without erasing the differences relevant to further work.

The return movement can then disclose further separations. Trying to integrate local results may reveal that apparent agreement concealed different assumptions. Trying to reuse a concept may reveal the need to distinguish agreement at one instance from agreement under variation.

That is precisely the “secondary separations” idea: integration does not merely reconnect previously separated pieces; it can teach us where another distinction is needed. Reciprocally, making that distinction can enable an integration that was previously unavailable.

Neither K nor M exclusively owns separation or integration. The proposed correspondence concerns how their respective operations make one another intelligible and usable.

## 4. The resulting picture is recursive, without making recurrence compulsory

We can now read BWR through this arrangement:

> Borrow enabling relations; work through explicit distinctions; return an articulated achievement; replay it through another situated activity.

Successful replay may preserve an existing possibility, solve a problem, or help constitute a changed context. It need not always generate novelty. But when it does change the context, the next movement starts somewhere different: previous understanding has become part of what later activity encounters and uses.

Your pre-philosophical intuition then acquires a particularly good form:

> The context does not need to command its inhabitants to understand it. Some inhabitants’ activities make understanding consequential; their shared understanding can, in turn, alter the conditions of inhabiting it.

The infinite’s “quest to be itself” can remain an interpretation of this circulation, without carrying the explanatory burden.

One terminology boundary should remain explicit: these are currently generative and regressive directions, not established categorical variances. In particular, both functors in Allow ⊣ Require are ordinarily covariant; being a right adjoint does not mean being contravariant.

The relation I would retain is therefore: pre-history follows the emergence of situated capacities; pre-philosophy follows their articulation of context; the separation/integration mirror describes how each movement preserves distinctions while enabling relations—and sometimes discovers new distinctions through those relations.

## 2. Claude’s two BWRs reveal two different responsibilities

The proposed comparison is approximately this:

|  | Logical reading | Expressionist reading |
|---|---|---|
| Starting conditions | Assumptions, resources, established constructions | Relations and powers through which situated activity is possible |
| Work | Deriving or constructing | Acting and being affected |
| What can continue | An achievement through further application | An activity or organization through maintained or transformed conditions |
| Central question | What licenses this reuse? | What enables this continuation? |

On the logical side, the implication example is precise: derive B under assumption A, discharge A to obtain A ⇒ B, then apply that result when A is supplied. Introduction/elimination harmony concerns whether those construction and use rules fit together. It does not by itself establish actual uptake, sustainable inquiry, or contextual renewal. Pfenning, Harmony.

On the expressionist side, conatus concerns a thing’s striving to persist, which Spinoza identifies with its actual essence. But “affect, be affected, persevere” is not already a borrow–work–return–replay cycle. It does not automatically specify a return, a transferable achievement, or replenishment of the conditions supporting others. Moreover, Spinoza attributes conatus to things generally, not only organisms or communicating agents. Ethics, III, propositions 6–8.

The valuable connection is therefore a question:

> Under what conditions does the reuse of achievements contribute to the continuation—and possible transformation—of the activities that make achievements possible?

That is close to Hoapa’s central concern. A proof can remain reusable while the community capable of understanding it disappears. Conversely, a community can maintain itself while producing little that others can independently reuse. Neither responsibility subsumes the other.

## 3. This gives the two infinities a role without assigning one to each BWR

I would not make “large infinity = expressionist BWR” and “infinitesimal = logical BWR.” Both readings encounter both concerns.

Within our proposed interpretation:

- The large pole asks how situated activity relates to enabling conditions that its own organization or account does not exhaust.
- The infinitesimal pole asks how a determinate organization can carry differences and possibilities not exhausted by its currently stabilized form.

Their meeting question becomes:

> How can something remain sufficiently determinate to act and be reused, while remaining sensitive to changes in itself and its relations?

That connects conatus with variation more carefully than identifying conatus with change. Perseverance sometimes requires resisting variation; sometimes it requires changing. Conversely, a possible variation need not support continuation.

This makes the infinitesimal interesting not because it is inherently creative or caring, but because it can help distinguish preserving a present configuration from preserving specified capacities to respond.

## 4. Kock–Lawvere could help articulate the connection—not certify it

Here there is a concrete mathematical prospect. A smooth interpretation can distinguish:

- an element in its present state;
- its infinitesimal variations;
- constraints relating those variations to other elements;
- whether those variations extend into admissible further activity.

Kock–Lawvere gives a precise language for the first-order part: a map from the basic infinitesimal object into the smooth line is determined by a value and a slope. But infinitesimal variation, a chosen dynamics, and successful continuation remain different structures. Kock, Synthetic Differential Geometry, §§I.1, I.7–8.

For Hoapa, the proposed bridge would be: logical documentation can carry guarantees about the variations and continuations relevant to the activity it describes. The activity supplies the stakes; the mathematics makes particular preservation claims expressible and checkable.

Deleuze’s interest in differential relations is relevant to this search: he uses them to investigate how relations disclose determinations not given by considering their terms separately. That is a philosophical resource, not evidence that Kock–Lawvere realizes his programme. Deleuze, lecture of 29 April 1980. In particular, the synthetic derivative is obtained through a unique coefficient—not by dividing by a nilpotent infinitesimal.

This also sharpens the mirror. We separate an activity’s conditions of continuation from the conditions under which its documented achievements may be reused. Then we investigate integrations through which each can support the other, without confusing a valid inference with a viable practice.

Their connection—and its capacity to sustain renewal—is a principal question of Hoapa, not something already supplied by their shared name.
