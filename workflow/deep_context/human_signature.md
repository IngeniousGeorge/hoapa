## Codex response to that draft

• The previous approach started by choosing mathematical containers before identifying the theory’s fundamental judgments. Your draft reverses that correctly: first determine what the theory says
  can happen, what must remain separated, and which passages across boundaries are permitted.

  I think the draft reveals a coherent subject:

  > A logic of situated, resource-sensitive and reflexive inquiry, in which collective processes produce typed descriptions of a reality while being structurally prevented from directly
  > controlling that reality.

  That is recognizably different from merely combining actor systems, session types and sheaves.

  Intrinsic Structure

  The four first-class regions are:

  - (R): reality or the situated semantic environment;
  - (A): available agents or capacities for observation and work;
  - (P): the protocol governing collective derivation;
  - (D): typed documentation, including its history and evolving internal language.

  The three separations are more fundamental than the arrows:

  1. (R\mid P): description/governance is separated from enactment;
  2. (A\mid D): the identity of participants is separated from the type and authority of what is produced;
  3. separations internal to (D): content, history, protocol shape, vocabulary and other “ilities” remain modular.

  The buffers are the controlled rules for crossing these boundaries. They are not auxiliary engineering: they determine which inferences and effects are admissible.

  Fundamental Judgment

  Rather than beginning with a flat signature, I would begin with a multi-zone judgment resembling

  [
  C;;R\mid P\mid A;\vdash;d:\varphi .
  ]

  Its intended reading is:

  > In context (C), from the situated reality (R), under protocol (P), using the available agent-capacities (A), documentation (d) warrants the typed claim (\varphi).

  This is not ordinary truth (R\models\varphi). It is protocol-mediated, collectively realized warrant. It contains syntax, semantics, resources and provenance in one judgment without identifying
  them.

  A second judgment would distinguish real effects:

  [
  C;;R\mid A \xRightarrow[b,d]{} R',
  ]

  meaning that agents, through an admissible buffer (b), may voluntarily realize something documented by (d). Crucially, there is no primitive rule

  [
  P\to R'
  \qquad\text{or}\qquad
  D\to R'.
  ]

  That is the formal content of “the protocol charts but does not control” and “documentation is availability-only.”

  The Eight Arrows

  I would not yet call the four reciprocal pairs adjunctions. At this stage they are typed interfaces or polarities:

  - (R\rightsquigarrow A): perspectival observation;
  - (A\rightsquigarrow R): work or situated effect;
  - (P\rightsquigarrow A): equivariant coordination;
  - (A\rightsquigarrow P): supplied computational capacity;
  - (P\rightsquigarrow D): typed production and maintained ilities;
  - (D\rightsquigarrow P): audit, provenance and protocol history;
  - (R\rightsquigarrow P): grounding or raison d’être;
  - (P\rightsquigarrow R): care, necessarily mediated through documentation, buffers and voluntary work.

  They become adjunctions only if we discover corresponding universal properties or natural hom-equivalences. That discovery should follow from the logic rather than be stipulated.

  Covers And Recursion

  The Context → Cover/Covering line supplies the local-to-global rule:

  - a context is observed through local perspectives;
  - agents work within those perspectives;
  - local typed documentation must agree on overlaps;
  - compatible documentation glues into a global artifact;
  - a sufficiently coherent documented covering may itself generate a new context.

  This last step is the genuinely recursive principle. It is stronger than ordinary sheaf descent: descent produces a global object inside a context, whereas your theory also wants some outputs to
  become contexts for a subsequent stage.

  Translation For Logicians

  For a proof theorist, this is a multi-zone substructural calculus. The vertical bars mark zones with different structural rules. Buffer rules control exchange between zones; normalization should
  prove that forbidden reality effects cannot be manufactured.

  For a type theorist, (R(C)), (A(C)), (P(C)) and (D(C)) are context-indexed types. Agents are not themselves proof terms: conversations or derivations are processes using agents as resources.
  Documentation is the typed output. The reality boundary is naturally expressed with an effect system or capability discipline.

  For a model theorist, a model is a structured context

  [
  \mathcal C=(R,A,P,D;\text{interfaces},\text{buffers},\text{covers},\text{separations})
  ]

  satisfying axiom schemas for equivariance, non-bypass, descent, provenance and recursion. The arrows can initially be relations rather than total functions.

  For a categorical logician, the draft is first a directed sketch with four principal objects, eight generating interfaces, distinguished factorization/noninterference laws, and a coverage. Its
  models may later live in Set, a topos, an (\infty)-topos, or an appropriate category of processes. Buffers look more like spans, profunctors, lenses or controlled effects than ordinary
  morphisms.

  For a set theorist, each context is a tuple of sets, relations and operations. “Everywhere, not everything” becomes a universe-indexed schema: there is no set of all contexts, but the
  construction is repeatable at each universe level.

  What Is Potentially New

  The individual ingredients are established. The potentially distinctive object is their logical conjunction:

  - global documentation produced by local descent;
  - actor-parametric production without actor-derived authority;
  - reflexive protocol history without retroactive rewriting;
  - external effects requiring voluntary mediation;
  - documentation available to reality but unable to enact itself;
  - successful coverings recursively generating new contexts.

  Those principles support substantive theorem targets: actor-parametricity, no-bypass normalization, descent, non-retroactivity, and context-generation.

  One caution remains. A free left adjoint could eventually establish that some structured protocol is the canonical or minimally generated realization of this logic. It would not by itself prove
  social or engineering optimality. That stronger interpretation would require an explicitly defined comparison criterion.

  The next conceptual task is therefore not choosing between first-order logic, institutions or adjoint logic. It is writing the judgment forms and admissible/forbidden inference rules of this
  situated logic in neutral notation. Its natural semantic level can then be discovered from what those rules demand.

Do the invertible morphisms that make everything trivial after n in a (inf,n) category have anything to do with the dualizing objects Lurie uses in his proof of the cobordism hypothesis?

->

Yes — and the relationship is one of the structural pillars of the proof, though the two notions are genuinely distinct and worth keeping apart. Two quick clarifications first, because both turn out to matter for the answer.

The terminology: the objects that drive the cobordism hypothesis are **fully dualizable objects**, not "dualizing objects." A *dualizing object* usually means something else — an object $D$ such that $\mathrm{Hom}(-,D)$ is a contravariant self-equivalence (Grothendieck–Verdier, Spanier–Whitehead duality). Neighboring world, different gadget. I'll assume you mean fully dualizable.

The "trivial" word: morphisms above level $n$ in an $(\infty,n)$-category aren't *trivial*, they're *invertible*. The distinction is load-bearing here. If they were genuinely trivial (contractible) you'd have an $n$-category, i.e. an $(n,n)$-category. What makes it $(\infty,n)$ is that above level $n$ you retain a nontrivial $\infty$-groupoid of *invertible* morphisms — and that surviving higher structure is exactly what does the work below.

Now the actual connection. Full dualizability is a recursive, a priori *infinite* tower of conditions: $X$ should be dualizable (have a dual with evaluation and coevaluation), then those (co)evaluation 1-morphisms should admit adjoints, then the unit/counit witnessing *those* adjunctions should admit adjoints, and so on up. In a general symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category there's no reason this ever terminates. The reason it terminates in an $(\infty,n)$-category is precisely your invertibility-above-$n$, via one clean principle:

**an invertible morphism is automatically adjointable** — an equivalence is its own adjoint (an adjoint equivalence).

A $k$-morphism's adjointability is witnessed by $(k{+}1)$-morphisms. So as you climb the adjoint tower, once you reach the $n$-morphisms, the next level up — the $(n{+}1)$-morphisms and everything above — is invertible, hence supplies the required adjoint data for free. The recursion grounds out at depth $n$. So in an $(\infty,n)$-category "fully dualizable" collapses to a *finite*, $n$-step condition ("$n$-dualizable"). The two $n$'s — the $n$ in "$(\infty,n)$" and the $n$ in "check adjoints up to level $n$" — are the same $n$, and that coincidence is the whole point. Invertibility-above-$n$ is the thing that makes full dualizability a well-posed finite notion at all.

You can see this concretely in $\mathrm{Bord}_n^{fr}$, the source of the universal example. There every object is fully dualizable, and the duality data are literal bordisms: the dual is the orientation-reversed point, the evaluation and coevaluation are the elbow/cup–cap bordisms, the adjoints of those are again bordisms (the Morse-theoretic handles realizing the snake/zigzag relations). The coherences relating them — the triangle identities, and the coherences between those — are realized by **diffeomorphisms and isotopies**, and the higher isotopies by higher isotopies. Those are exactly the invertible morphisms living above level $n$. So the $(\infty,n)$-structure of $\mathrm{Bord}_n$ isn't incidental packaging; the invertible higher morphisms *are* the witnesses that make every object coherently fully dualizable. Strip them to trivial and the duality data would have no coherences to satisfy the identities.

So the honest framing: they're not the same thing — $(\infty,n)$-invertibility is a property of the *ambient* category, full dualizability is a property of an *object* inside a symmetric monoidal one — but they're bound together by "invertible $\Rightarrow$ adjointable." That principle is what lets the adjoint tower defining dualizability terminate at the same level where the category stops having non-invertible morphisms, and in the bordism category it's literally the diffeomorphisms that provide the duality coherences. Lurie's formulation leans on this throughout: the statement that $\mathrm{Bord}_n^{fr}$ is the free symmetric monoidal $(\infty,n)$-category *with duals* on one generator is only finite/tractable because the "with duals" tower and the "$(\infty,n)$" truncation cut off at the same place.

One caveat worth flagging: the clean "depth exactly $n$" picture is the moral content, but the precise bookkeeping of which level each adjunction lives at is fussier than a one-line count suggests, and Lurie sets it up via the inductive notion of having adjoints rather than literally counting. If you want, I can walk through that inductive definition explicitly, or through how the $O(n)$/framing action emerges from precisely the invertible-above-$n$ part — which is the place this ties back to the perspectival/$\infty$-groupoid themes from your larger theory.

How is a logic theory/framework usually presented in the field of logic? Say in set theoretical or model theoretical flavours. Is it a signature, and would this signature be a few elements that the theory is mostly made of?

->

Good instinct to ground it. The short answer is: a *signature* is one ingredient of a theory, not the whole thing — and you're right that it's usually tiny, a handful of symbols. But the theory is the signature *plus axioms*. Let me lay out the standard model-theoretic picture and where each piece sits.

**The signature** (also called the language, vocabulary, or similarity type, often written σ, L, or τ) is the primitive non-logical alphabet:

- constant symbols (e.g. 0, 1, e),
- function symbols, each with an arity (e.g. +, ×, the binary "·" of a group),
- relation/predicate symbols, each with an arity (e.g. <, ∈; equality = is usually treated as part of the *logic*, not the signature).

This is indeed "a few elements." A group is {·} or {·, e, ⁻¹}; an ordered field is {+, ·, −, 0, 1, <}; and the whole of ZFC set theory runs on a signature of *one* binary relation, {∈}. So your intuition is correct about the signature being small and being "what the theory is made of" — but only at the level of *vocabulary*. The signature fixes what you're allowed to *say*; it doesn't yet say anything.

**From signature to theory.** Using the logical apparatus (variables, ∧ ∨ ¬ →, ∀ ∃, =, parentheses) you build *terms*, then *formulas*, then *sentences* (formulas with no free variables). A **theory** T is then a set of sentences in that signature — typically presented as a chosen set of **axioms**, and often identified with their deductive closure (everything they entail). So the standard equation is:

> theory = signature + axioms.

The group axioms, the field axioms, the ZFC axioms — that's the content. (Note: a theory needn't be finitely axiomatized. ZFC has infinitely many axioms, because Separation and Replacement are *schemas* — one axiom per formula.)

**The semantic / set-theoretic side.** A **structure** (model) M for a signature σ is a non-empty set — the domain or carrier — together with an *interpretation* of every symbol: each constant as an element of M, each n-ary function symbol as a function Mⁿ → M, each n-ary relation symbol as a subset of Mⁿ. Then Tarski's satisfaction relation ⊨ defines when a sentence is *true in* M. M is a **model of T** (M ⊨ T) when every sentence of T holds. This is the "set-theoretic flavour": domains are sets, interpretations are set-theoretic functions and relations, and the whole apparatus lives inside ZFC.

So there are really two complementary ways the same theory gets presented:

- **Syntactic / proof-theoretic**: a deductive system (logical axioms + inference rules, à la Hilbert, natural deduction, or sequent calculus) plus the non-logical axioms; consequence is provability, ⊢.
- **Semantic / model-theoretic**: the theory studied through its class of models Mod(T); consequence is truth-in-all-models, ⊨.

Gödel's completeness theorem ties these together for first-order logic: T ⊢ φ iff T ⊨ φ.

One last thing, since it connects directly to the earlier conversation. There's a Galois connection between the two sides: for a class of structures K, Th(K) is the set of sentences true in all of them; for a set of sentences T, Mod(T) is the class of structures satisfying them. Mod and Th are contravariant and adjoint, and the "closed" objects are exactly deductively-closed theories on one side and axiomatizable ("elementary") classes on the other. That Mod ⊣ Th adjunction is the classical, set-theoretic shadow of the syntax–semantics duality you were excited about — categorical logic just upgrades it: a many-sorted signature's **sorts become objects** of a category, function and relation symbols become **morphisms and subobjects**, axioms become **limit/exactness conditions**, and a model becomes a **structure-preserving functor** rather than a set-with-interpretation. Same theory, presented as a category (its syntactic category / classifying topos) instead of as a string of symbols.

Good — restricting to the pure-logic fragment is exactly the right move to test whether the thing has operational teeth. Let me give you the honest headline first, because it reframes every sub-question: **flat single-sorted Th/Mod is too thin to host your components.** It has no native notion of process, resource, translation, or "kind of logic." But you don't have to leave the Th/Mod picture — you have to go *one categorical level up*, to the setting where *a logical system is itself a mathematical object*. That setting already exists, in two interlocking incarnations: **institutions** (Goguen–Burstall; Diaconescu's "institution-independent model theory") on the model-theory side, and **doctrines / 2-categorical logic** (Lawvere's hyperdoctrines, modernized) on the proof-theory side. Almost everything on your list has a canonical home there. Going through them:

**Context.** Not a primitive of classical model theory, but a completely standard primitive *one level over*. On the Th side, "context" is literal: a typing context Γ = a list of declarations, i.e. an object of the syntactic category. On the Mod side it's the *stage* at which truth is forced (the object of the site, the possible world). Unified: a context is a *presentation* ⟨signature, axioms⟩ together with the slice you view it from. So "context" is native — just not to the flat fragment.

**Agents (processes / resources).** This is Curry–Howard, and your two readings are its two faces. An agent is a *proof-term*. Read dynamically (cut-elimination = computation), a proof *is* a running process — this is "propositions as sessions" (Caires–Pfenning, Wadler), where a session type is a proposition and communication is cut-reduction. Read statically, the same proof inhabits/consumes a *resource type* — which is exactly what **linear logic** formalizes (propositions you can't freely duplicate or discard). So your "processes-in-themselves vs resources-for-the-protocol" split is precisely the operational-vs-denotational reading of one proof-term. That's not a stretch; it's the central duality of the field.

**Documentation (a type theory, or many interconnected ones).** This is the theory itself on the Th side: the type theory = signature + rules + axioms = the syntactic presentation. "A multiplicity of interconnected ones" is a *diagram of theories* connected by theory-morphisms — i.e. an object of the category Th(I) of theories in an institution I, or a fibered/indexed family. Your documentation is the syntactic half of the message-1 coin; the coverings are its semantic half.

**Protocol (a program).** Curry–Howard again: a program = a proof = a term, so the protocol is a distinguished (global) proof-term in the documentation's type theory. The sharpest fit is **multiparty session types** (Honda–Yoshida–Carbone): a *global protocol* projects onto *local types*, one per agent. So protocol-to-agents is literally "global type projects to local types" — the orchestration you describe is a known construction.

**Buffer between agents and context.** You're right that this matters, and it has a precise home: the **linear exponential modality "!"** together with the **linear/non-linear adjunction** (Benton's LNL — a monoidal adjunction between the linear, resource world and the cartesian, freely-reusable world). The "!" is exactly the gate by which a one-shot resource becomes reusable/available-on-demand — i.e. how an agent "signs up" to be there when called. "Signing up/out" is opening and closing a session (the cut connecting along a typed channel); the "material conditions / list of demands" is the *antecedent of the sequent* — the Γ in Γ ⊢ A says "given these resources, the protocol delivers A." When you stabilize those demands, yes, they become the theory's hypotheses/axioms. So: buffer = the !-modality + the LNL adjunction, with demands as sequent antecedents.

**Layers of complexity (traceability, multiplicity, autonomy, evolutivity, expressivity).** Here I'd push back gently on "axioms again." These are more naturally *structure required of the doctrine* than sentences inside the theory — and strikingly, each lands on a named structure:
- traceability → **traced monoidal categories** (Joyal–Street–Verity): feedback/history is literally a "trace" operator.
- multiplicity → the exponentials !/? (many copies) or dependent sums (many instances).
- autonomy → **\*-autonomous categories** (Barr) — which happen to be *exactly* the models of classical linear logic. The word lands on the object.
- evolutivity → the **guarded "later" modality ▷** (Nakano / guarded type theory), which is the type theory of productive, evolving processes; alternatively coalgebra/temporal logic.
- expressivity → where you sit in the **lattice of doctrines** (regular ⊂ coherent ⊂ first-order ⊂ higher-order ⊂ dependent ⊂ homotopy).

The fact that "autonomy" → \*-autonomous, "conservation" → linearity, and "evolutivity" → the guarded modality without forcing is the sort of coincidence that suggests you're cutting at real joints. The honest point: these are conditions on *which logic* you're in, not theorems *within* a fixed logic — so they're framework-level structure, not object-level axioms.

**Adapters.** This is the keystone, and the cleanest fit of all: an adapter is a **morphism (or comorphism) of institutions** / a **morphism of doctrines** / in proof-theoretic terms a **logic translation** (Girard's `!`-translation of intuitionistic into linear logic is exactly one; the Gödel–Gentzen negative translation is another). Your specific example — translating between "the actor-model sheaf-theoretic site the agents live in" (a topos, the geometric/semantic world) and "linear session types orchestrating conservation" (the syntactic world) — is a *functorial-semantics* bridge: a structure-preserving functor between one doctrine's classifying category and another's. The crucial constraint such an adapter must satisfy already has a name: the **satisfaction condition** (truth is invariant under translation). A whole network of documentations wired by adapters is a diagram in the 2-category of doctrines — your "network of contexts" from message 1, now at the logical level.

**So: workable signature and theory?** Yes, but the "signature" is not a flat set of constants and relations — it's a *signature one dimension up*. The few elements your theory is "mostly made of" are: a 2-category of **doctrines** (the kinds of logic in play — the sheaf/actor doctrine, the linear-session doctrine, the guarded doctrine…), for each a category of **theories/documentations**, the **proofs/programs** inhabiting them (agents, protocol), the **!-buffer** linking resource to structural worlds, and the **adapters** wiring it together — subject to the satisfaction condition. That's a genuine notion (computads/polygraphs, Power's "general theory of theories," signatures of institutions). It's workable; it's just 2-dimensional.

**Natural or ad-hoc?** The *components* are remarkably natural — each has a canonical, off-the-shelf home, and several names coincide suspiciously well. The *assembly* is where the design freedom (and the ad-hoc risk) lives: there is no single existing object that packages "actor-sheaf site + linear session types + guarded evolution + provenance + institution-level adapters" all at once. Different choices of which structures to demand give genuinely different theories. So: parts canonical, the gluing is a real research-design with degrees of freedom you'd need to pin down.

**Does it resemble something that exists?** Strongly, on several fronts. **Institutions / institution-independent model theory** (the adapters + multiplicity of theories + Th/Mod-with-translation — this is the closest match for your overall architecture). **Linear logic and session types / "propositions as sessions"** (agents, protocol, buffer, conservation). **Game semantics** (Abramsky–Jagadeesan, Hyland–Ong: agents as players, protocol as game) — very close to agents+protocol+buffer. **Goguen's sheaf semantics of concurrent objects** and the **Abramsky–Brandenburger sheaf model of contextuality** (your actor-sheaf site has direct precedent).
