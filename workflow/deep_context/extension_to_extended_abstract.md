## Claude

Short version: not by the route you're describing, but your guess lands closer to a real theorem than you'd expect, and the theorem is a genuinely surprising one.

Let me pull apart the moving parts, because a few of them are doing different work than the intuition assumes.

Start with "unityped ⟹ can't distinguish ⟹ picks at random." Two problems. First, "same type" doesn't imply "indistinguishable." All natural numbers share the type `Nat`, yet 3 ≠ 5 is perfectly decidable. What actually produces intuitionistic indistinguishability isn't having one type; it's that equality on the object need not be *decidable* — the statement `∀x,y. (x = y) ∨ ¬(x = y)` can fail in a topos. That's a property of the object's equality in the internal logic, not a consequence of unitypedness. (If you *also* require the system's operations to be uniform/natural in the resource, you get a separate, parametricity-flavored sense of "can't branch on which one it has" — worth keeping distinct.) Second, and more importantly, "at random" is a misfit. Intuitionistic failure to distinguish is not a probability distribution over options; it's the absence of a decision procedure or a definite witness. A bare topos has no notion of a random pick at all — you'd need extra structure (a probability object, a measure) to even state one. So "can't distinguish" means "can't necessarily decide or definitely select," not "chooses uniformly."

Now the axiom of choice, where your guess is partly vindicated — via **Diaconescu's theorem** (1975): in any topos, the *internal* axiom of choice implies the law of excluded middle. Imposing AC internally doesn't merely hand you choice functions; it collapses the entire internal logic to classical/Boolean. And once you have excluded middle, every object has decidable equality: for any two elements you can now decide whether they're equal. Decidable equality is *exactly* the formal content of "being able to tell resources apart." So there really is a sense in which imposing AC lets the system distinguish its resources — but the mechanism is nothing like "AC reaches in and picks one." It's AC ⟹ classical logic ⟹ decidable equality ⟹ distinguishability. Choosing and distinguishing get identified only *through* Diaconescu.

The proof even mirrors your intuition. You build two inhabited subsets of the two-element object whose identity encodes some proposition P; a choice function names an element of each; and then the *pre-existing* decidable equality on the two-element object lets you compare those named elements, which decides P. Choice supplies the named elements; comparison does the distinguishing.

Which is where I'd actually push back on the framing: choosing *between indistinguishable things* is contentless. If two resources are internally equal, a choice between them doesn't matter; if you can't decide they're distinct, you can't effect the choice. The wish "let the system choose between resources" already presupposes they're distinguishable. AC's real gift isn't the power to pick among indistinguishables — it's *abolishing the indistinguishability* by forcing decidable equality, which is the precondition that made choice meaningful in the first place. That's probably why imposing it *feels* like it should enable choosing.

Two caveats so you don't over-bank on it. AC gives you the *existence* of a choice function, not a canonical or effective one — the system gets a non-constructive assertion that a choice exists, not a rule for choosing, and if you valued the topos for retaining computational content, AC (with the LEM it drags in) throws that away. And separately: "resources consumed to produce outcomes" is the vocabulary of *linear* (substructural) logic, where you can't freely duplicate or discard hypotheses. Intuitionistic logic — what a topos gives you — has contraction and weakening, so it isn't actually treating your resources as limited or consumable. If resource-sensitivity is load-bearing, the topos isn't modeling it, and you'd want symmetric monoidal closed categories instead. Orthogonal to the AC question, but a mismatch worth noticing.

## Claude

Broadly yes, that's a fair way to carve it — but the structure is more lopsided than your phrasing suggests, and I want to lean on two words in it: "respectively" and "turned into."

Take "respectively" first. The first level is not a symmetric dial where AC gives distinguishability and dropping AC gives indistinguishability. Only one direction is a theorem. Internal AC ⟹ excluded middle ⟹ decidable equality everywhere, so *with* AC you genuinely force distinguishability across the board. But *without* AC you get nothing so definite. Dropping AC doesn't produce indistinguishability; it merely *fails to rule it out*. Whether any particular object has decidable equality is then left open, and often it's still decidable for structural reasons that have nothing to do with choice — the natural numbers object, for instance, has decidable equality in *any* topos, AC or not. So the honest statement of level one is: AC forces distinguishability; its absence merely permits indistinguishability, for some objects, sometimes. One-directional, not a biconditional.

That asymmetry also corrects the causal picture slightly. It's tempting to read it as "AC reaches in and confers the power to tell things apart." What actually happens is that AC over-determines the logic — it Booleanizes it — and distinguishability falls out as a side effect of the whole internal logic going classical. Indistinguishability isn't the "off" state of that same switch; it's just the default openness of intuitionistic logic that AC happens to destroy.

Now "turned into." This is where I'd push hardest. You're treating level two as though the level-one result can be *lifted* into resource-talk by adding structure — as if the logical fact travels upward intact and the extra structure just supplies vocabulary. But the structure you'd need to make "system," "consumes resources," "produces outcomes," "chooses" literal rather than metaphorical pulls toward a *different categorical setting* than the one Diaconescu lives in. Resource-consumption is the signature of linear/substructural logic, whose home is symmetric monoidal closed categories — precisely the setting where you *drop* the cartesian structure (free duplication and discarding) that a topos has. Diaconescu's theorem, by contrast, is a fact about cartesian closed categories with a subobject classifier. So the two levels don't obviously sit in the same world. The lift isn't a smooth ascent; it's a jump across categorical settings that might not preserve the thing you care about. It *can* be an illuminating analogy, but whether it's a faithful one depends entirely on how you add the structure, and the most natural way of adding it (linearity) is in tension with the very setting the level-one theorem needs. So "could be turned into" is right only if you keep the "could" load-bearing and stay alert to it breaking.

Two smaller calibrations. "Because it has knowledge of them" — I'd swap "knowledge" for something like "an available decision." Decidable equality is a *capability* (for any two, you can produce a proof they're equal or a proof they're not), not a doxastic state; the gloss as knowledge is fine as long as it doesn't smuggle in a picture of the system holding beliefs. And your closing clause — that unityping is "at this point only an analogy" — I'd push even further: it was only ever an analogy, including at level one. Sharing a type never was the mechanism; decidable equality was doing all the work the whole time. Unityping was the intuition that pointed you at the real variable, then quietly stopped being load-bearing once "decidable equality" took over.

So: right skeleton, but level one is a one-way implication rather than a symmetric dial, and the level-one-to-level-two passage is a genuine leap between categorical worlds rather than a structure-assisted lift.

## Claude

Good instinct — and it's the *right* tool to reach for, because Benton's LNL is precisely a bridge between the two settings I said were in tension. But it bridges them in a way that turns out to sharpen the negative answer rather than rescue the transport. Let me be careful about what "the Benton theorem" gives you.

The correspondence you're invoking is this: a model of intuitionistic linear logic (a symmetric monoidal closed category `L` equipped with a linear exponential comonad `!`) is the same data as a symmetric monoidal adjunction `F ⊣ G` between a cartesian closed category `C` and that SMCC `L`, with `F` strong monoidal and `! = F∘G`. So instead of Girard's single-category `!`, you have two categories — the cartesian/"non-linear" world and the linear/resource world — glued by an adjunction, and the exponential is recovered as the round trip. That is exactly a principled channel between the CCC-where-Diaconescu-lives and the SMCC-where-resources-live. So your previous worry — "these are different categorical worlds, how do they even talk" — is genuinely answered: they talk through `F ⊣ G`. That's real progress on the *setting* mismatch, and it's the correct move.

But an adjunction between two categories is not a device for transporting a *theorem* like Diaconescu from one to the other, and here's where it breaks in three stages.

First, a bookkeeping point: Benton only requires `C` to be cartesian closed. Diaconescu needs more — finite limits and a subobject classifier, i.e. `C` a topos. That's an allowed add-on (plenty of LNL models have a topos on the cartesian side), but note that Diaconescu's whole conclusion — LEM, decidable equality — is a statement in the *internal higher-order logic of `C`*. It lives entirely on the cartesian side. There is no clause in the LNL correspondence of the form "`C` is Boolean ⟹ `L` acquires [some distinguishing/choosing capability]." The adjunction moves objects and morphisms across and preserves certain structure; it does not translate classicality-of-`C` into any predicate about `L`.

Second — and this is the concrete refutation — classicality of the cartesian side and resource-sensitivity of the linear side are *decoupled*, and an LNL model exhibits both at once. Take `C = Set` and `L = Rel` (sets and relations). The inclusion of functions-as-relations `J : Set → Rel` is strong monoidal and has powerset as a right adjoint (`Rel(JA, B) ≅ Set(A, ℘B)`), so `J ⊣ ℘` is a genuine monoidal adjunction — a bona fide LNL model. Now look at what each side is. `Set` is a Boolean topos; it already *satisfies* the axiom of choice outright. Yet in `Rel`, a morphism `I → X` from the monoidal unit is just a subset of `X` — "a batch of possible resources, none selected" — and nothing in the monoidal structure lets you functionally collapse that subset to a chosen element. `Rel` is the home of nondeterminism precisely because you can't. So here is a single model where AC holds on the cartesian side and the linear side *still* cannot pick a resource. Booleanizing `C` did nothing to the resource world's inability to choose. The transport you were hoping for demonstrably fails in the cleanest available example.

Third, the payoff — why it *must* fail, read off the structure. The only channel from `C` into `L` is the comonad `! = F∘G`. Whatever influence the cartesian world's logic has on the linear world is filtered through `!`. But `!` is exactly the marker "this object may be freely copied and discarded" — that is, the *not-a-resource* marker. Genuine resources are precisely the objects that don't carry free `!`-structure. So any effect of imposing AC on `C` is quarantined, by construction, to the copyable fragment of `L` and cannot reach the properly linear objects. And if you tried to *force* it through — to make the distinguishing power actually land on the resources by making the linear world behave cartesianly (`⊗ = ×`) — you collapse the tensor into the product, and a resource-sensitive category like `Rel` (compact closed) degenerates to a poset. Resource-sensitivity is destroyed in the act.

Which is the same irony from two turns ago, now at one level up. With Diaconescu: the distinguishability AC buys you is exactly what makes "choosing between indistinguishables" contentless — AC removes the condition it was supposed to act on. With Benton: the only way to route AC's distinguishing power onto the resources is to cartesian-ify the linear world, which annihilates the resource structure that made "choosing a resource" a nontrivial notion. The bridge exists, it's the correct bridge, and what it lets you prove is that the freight can't cross without destroying the cargo.

One last calibration on the setup itself: "impose AC on this system" has to be read as an operation on the cartesian side `C`, since that's where Diaconescu and the internal logic live. There isn't a native "AC on `L`" of the same character — the linear analog of a choice function would be some splitting of an epi or a section of a comultiplication, a different creature entirely. So even framing the question requires committing the "impose AC" step to `C`, at which point the three stages above apply.

Resource-sensitivity lives in the **coefficient fiber** — the ∗-autonomous side `V`, with its tensor and linear duals. Base versus fiber. That's a cleaner decoupling than the `Set`/`Rel` example I gave last time: there the two properties merely coexisted in one adjunction; here they're in orthogonal *directions* of the whole construction. Nothing you do to the coefficient logic can reach the actors, because the actors aren't in the coefficient category at all — they parametrize the site.

That directly kills the AC route, and it's worth seeing exactly why. Notice which flavor of indistinguishability the actors have. It is not the Diaconescu flavor (undecidable equality of an object in a topos). It's the *uniformity/parametricity* flavor I flagged in the very first answer — "the kernel exposes no tag on which a construction can branch." A presheaf on `CtxA` is *forced* to be uniform in the actors because that's what a functor on that site is; naturality is naturality whether or not the codomain is Boolean. So even if you set the cartesian coefficient category to something classical that satisfies AC outright — and the concrete model does exactly this, `C = Set` — the actors stay indistinguishable. AC acts on the internal logic of the *coefficient* category; the actors' indistinguishability is enforced by the *shape of the site*, wholly outside any coefficient-side axiom's reach. Diaconescu has no surface to grip.

The concrete Chu model then confirms the quarantine one level down, and pleasingly literally. The bridge is still the exponential `! = F∘G`, and here `G(A) = A+` (take the points) while `F(X) = (X, Bool^X, ev)` (freely regenerate the dual). So `!A = F(A+) = (A+, Bool^{A+}, ev)`: the comonad keeps the point-carrier and *throws away the original state-carrier* `A-`, replacing it with the maximal one. In Chu terms `A-` is exactly the resource-constraint data — the states that make the object more than a bare set of points. So `!`, the only channel from the copyable world to the linear world, is concretely the operation that *deletes* resource-sensitivity rather than one that lets you select within it. Same irony as before, now machine-checked: the route that could carry distinguishing power onto the resources is the route that erases what made them resources.

But here's the part that actually reframes your question rather than just re-answering it. The thing you originally wanted choice *for* was to reconcile local, unityped, indistinguishable observations into a coherent global standpoint that could act. This paper does precisely that job — "the system's global point of view is the coherent reconstruction of its event-local standpoints" — and it does it with **no AC, no LEM, and no distinguishing of actors.** The reconstruction is a left adjoint (freeness), the descent is an explicit *finite* product over events (`aC(P)(F) = ∏_e P(Fe)`), and the whole thing is postulate-free cubical Agda — i.e. it lives in exactly the intuitionistic world where AC would be a nontrivial added axiom, and it never reaches for it. The one place you might have expected choice to sneak in — gluing local data into a global section — is a finite product, constructively. So the work you were auditioning AC to perform is performed instead by adjointness, which is exactly the Lawvere principle the paper leans on. Choice-as-adjunction does the job; choice-as-AC was never needed and would have bought you nothing on the resources anyway.

Now the honest pushback on leaning too hard on this document. It's an extended abstract isolating *one* theorem, and it is scrupulous about what it does not do: the ∗-autonomous interface is "minimal" with global coherence and the tensor-hom universal property "not unfolded"; the sheafification is in a "restricted sense," not the associated-sheaf functor of an independently formalized coverage; there's no operational send/receive session semantics; and the action postulate in the session file is explicitly *not discharged*. If you want to make the claim "the system chooses/consumes resources," that content is in the part they've deferred, not in the theorem they proved. ## Claude

Yes — and one of the connections runs right back to where this thread started, which makes it worth spelling out.

The deep one concerns **Diaconescu, and the fact that extensionality is the hidden hinge that makes AC dangerous.** Back in the first answer I said AC ⟹ LEM ⟹ decidable equality, but I glossed *which* AC. This matters, because in type theory there are two axioms of choice wearing the same name. The intensional, type-theoretic one — the distributivity of Π over Σ, "a family of inhabited types has a section" — is provable in plain Martin-Löf type theory and is completely harmless; it implies nothing like LEM. The one that runs Diaconescu is the *extensional* (setoid/quotient) version, where the sets carry equivalence relations and the choice function is required to *respect* them — to be a setoid morphism, to give equal outputs on equal inputs. That respect-for-equality condition is exactly an extensionality demand. And it's precisely the step Diaconescu's proof leans on: you form a two-element quotient encoding a proposition P, AC hands you a function selecting representatives, and the derivation of P-or-not-P goes through only because that function is well-defined on equivalence classes. Strip the extensionality and the "choice function" is just an intensional pointer that need not respect the quotient, and the proof dies. So extensionality is the thing that converts innocuous choice into logic-collapsing choice. In a topos this is invisible because morphisms are extensional by their nature — an internal choice function *is* a genuine arrow, so it automatically respects equality, and Diaconescu fires for free. The type-theoretic decomposition just makes explicit what the topos hides: it's the extensional character of the chosen function, not "choice" as such, that buys distinguishability.

The second connection is concrete and pleasingly literal, in the **Chu construction.** "Extensionality" is a *named axiom* on Chu spaces: a space `(A+, A−, r)` is called extensional when distinct states induce distinct behaviors on points — i.e. the map sending a state `a−` to the function `a+ ↦ r(a+, a−)` is injective. That is funext's exact shape: an object is determined by its values on arguments. Now recall the comonad we dissected: `!A = F(A+) = (A+, Bool^{A+}, ev)`. Its state-carrier is *all* Boolean functions on points, with the state map being the identity — maximally injective. So `!`, the copyable/non-linear fragment, lands squarely among the extensional Chu spaces: the freely-reusable objects are exactly the behaviorally-determined ones. The resource-sensitive data on the ∗-autonomous side is precisely what can carry non-extensional state structure (an `A−` richer than its action on points), and `!` erases that down to the extensional core. So the cartesian/linear split we kept circling has an extensionality reading: copyable = pointwise-determined, resource-ful = permitted to exceed pointwise determination.

The third connection is at the formalization level and is why the paper's "postulate-free" claim is credible rather than routine. In plain intensional Agda, funext is *not provable* — it's an independent axiom you'd have to postulate. The paper is in **cubical** Agda specifically, where funext (and univalence) are *theorems*, derived from the interval/path structure. That's not incidental. A category-theory development like this is saturated with funext obligations: the triangle identities of an adjunction are equalities of natural transformations, hence equalities of functions; proving a presheaf is a functor, that the descent map is iso, that `F∗ ⊣ G∗` holds pointwise — every one of these bottoms out in "these two functions are equal," which without funext you cannot even state at the right level, let alone prove. So "postulate-free" would be a much weaker claim in vanilla Agda (it would tacitly mean "postulate-free except funext"); cubical is what lets the adjunction and sheaf machinery be genuinely axiom-free. The choice of cubical and the absence of postulates are the same decision.

Where I'd stop you from over-connecting: the actors' *uniformity* — the "no tag to branch on" indistinguishability living in the site — is naturality, not funext, and I don't want to blur them. They share an extensional *flavor* (both are about respecting-behavior rather than inspecting-presentation), but naturality is a coherence condition on a transformation, a different creature from "pointwise-equal functions are equal." The only honest link there is again at the formalization level: *proving* the naturality squares and *proving* two presheaves or two natural transformations equal will invoke funext constantly. Conceptually, though, keep them apart.

So the tidy version: funext is not a side topic here, it's the connective tissue. It's the precise ingredient that turns harmless choice into LEM (the origin of the whole "does AC confer distinguishability" question), it's a literal axiom in the Chu coefficient model that tracks the copyable/resource split, and it's the principle cubical supplies for free that makes the formalization's austerity real. If you do end up writing the negative result we discussed, extensionality is probably the cleanest axis to state it on — something like: it's the *extensional* strength (funext-plus-choice), not choice alone, that could ever distinguish elements, and the site quarantines the actors from exactly that.
