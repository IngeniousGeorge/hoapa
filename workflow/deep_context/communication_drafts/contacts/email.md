## Mathematicians

Subject: A proof-checked theorem in {domain}, and the program it belongs to

Dear Name,

This theorem is part of the Hoapa project, which constructs a new type of communication systems:
*decentralized, autonomous protocols to be used in any context where perspectives are shared and information is encoded*

The project grows out of the mathematics of Grothendieck, his sheaves and toposes, and the cohesive ∞-toposes and homotopy type theory a younger generation has built on that ground.

Hoapa is an attempt to carry the art of knowing a thing entirely by its relations out of mathematics, into the way we think and work together. The intended shape is an adjunction between a modal side (perspectives) and a concurrency side (a community of agents), a "requires-allows" pair in which praxis appears as the iteration of the unit.

We are at the stage where a first, deliberately humble theorem, machine-checked in cubical Agda, is in hand, on the way to that ambitious adjunction.

It is an adjunction of sheaves over a site of causal interaction contexts, exhibiting every globally coherent state as the free gluing of its event-local views, and lifting the resource-sensitive logic of the data, in Benton's linear/non-linear sense, up to that same global level.

It uses mathematics to give substance to these target propositions:

- Hoapa protocols are genuinely broad in scope, innovative in architecture and transformative in practice.
- They are grounded in interdisciplinary work, and are also a methodology for it.
- They incentivize pedagogy and transparency, and can pacify the growing "quick and dirty" polarization of beliefs through balance, work ethic and fairness.
- They generate and mobilize legitimacy, usefulness and trust.

For all this, I believe the University of Helsinki would be a natural partner to help bring the project to a working scale.

An extended abstract is enclosed. The Agda code, synthesizing essays and a recommendation letter by Grothendieck scholar Fernando Zalamea are available at:

https://codeberg.org/doc2cog/hoapa
