### The mirror: separation, integration, and reciprocal induced structure

This formulation supersedes the earlier separation-to-separation sketch in
this synthesis. Investigate whether **separations of concerns in K enable
structural capacities for integration in M**, and whether M's integration
capabilities induce further operational distinctions, especially within
documentation. Unification may mean a coherent family, translation system,
common interface, or reconstruction; it need not merge everything into one
object. The correspondence can be productive precisely by preserving differences.

"Secondary" means induced through the proposed correspondence, not less
important or later in time. Starting from K, ask which contextual unifications
its separations enable; starting from M, ask which separations are needed to
realize its integration capabilities in organized work. These directions do
not require a second adjunction, and a property induced in one investigation
can organize the next. Neither direction is yet a theorem about Hoapa.

The reality/system-of-understanding boundary is the **surface that both
separates and connects**. An account neither becomes nor exhausts its subject,
but must remain answerable to it through observation, testing, interpretation,
and action. Above/below is an organizing image, not a priority of being, a
temporal arrow, or a prohibition on intervention. The intended process is
co-constitutive. The Wheeler-style reciprocal-constraint image is an aid, not
a gravitational model. M must describe independently meaningful contextual
structure, not just commentary on K; it does not formalize all Reality.
The mathematical action may concern documentation and its interfaces without
modeling participants' interior lives.

**Perspectivism remains an open relation.** Distinguish available contextual
variation, the perspectives K can access and articulate, and new perspectives
made possible by its work. Investigate their interaction without asserting
that K produces all perspectivism in M or merely receives a fixed inventory.

**Two realms of understanding.** The stated project interpretation is that
embodied understanding is organized predominantly synthetically through
unityped participation, and documentation predominantly analytically through
typed concepts and relations. Both moments occur in both realms; documentation
can furnish synthetic languages for later work. The political counterpressure
is to turn documentary distinctions into fixed kinds and ranks of people.
See [the canonical Lautman account and architectural counterexample](../related_structures.md#lautman--analyticsynthetic-decomposition-and-recursive-incarnation).
This is not an assignment of synthetic/analytic to M/K or of the two kinds of
equality to agents/concepts.

#### Starting from K: candidate secondary unifications on M

- **Contributor versus contribution:** can a producer-independent documentary
  organization connect work across identities and specialties? Compare it
  with output-dependent participant standing under the same task and uses.
- **Episode versus artifact:** can different episodes contribute to one
  reusable context even when producers are unavailable? Compare independent
  reuse with an interface requiring renewed producer authorization.
- **Local vocabulary versus explicit translation:** can a coherent network
  preserve local vocabularies? Compare declared translations with compulsory
  adoption of one vocabulary, under the same required distinctions.
- **Causal dependence versus conceptual equivalence:** can integration retain
  both explanatory history and structural comparison? Test a directed
  dependency that would incorrectly become reversible if the two were merged.

Each needs specified carriers, a construction, and a preservation/comparison
law. These questions concern contextual capabilities, not just preferences
among architectures. Positive results do not establish necessity; a rival
may support the same use by another mechanism.

#### What an adjunction would supply, and what remains to construct

For `L = Allow` and `R = Require`, an adjunction would provide the natural
correspondence `K(Lm, k) ≅ M(m, Rk)`, the unit and counit, and the monad `RL`
and comonad `LR`. These support systematic passage and comparison, not an
automatic identification of either round trip with the identity. Left
adjoints preserve colimits and right adjoints preserve limits; once a proposed
integration is characterized by an appropriate universal property, such
preservation can do genuine work. Other structures need additional laws.
[Riehl, *Category Theory in Context*, chapters 4–5](https://math.jhu.edu/~eriehl/context.pdf).

The open task is to make separation and integration precise enough that the
secondary effects become consequences of a constructed correspondence.
Adjointness alone names none of these disciplines. The examples do not
construct Allow/Require, force a second pair, or decide their polarity.
Protected distinctions, inadequate observations, and failed future-use
preservation remain different: matching lists prove no equivalence, and
non-matching lists do not by themselves measure Anthropic loss.

### Generation and description — lead compatibility investigation

Monads with arities is the lead established template to examine. Given a
monad T on a category E with suitable arities A, the associated theory ΘT
uses free T-algebras on those arities. The nerve theorem gives a fully
faithful nerve of T-algebras and characterizes its essential image among
presheaves on ΘT. In familiar cases this is a Segal-type condition.
[Berger–Melliès–Weber, Theorem 1.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.3064).

This links free/forgetful structure to coherent description; it does not say
the two adjunctions are literally identical or have identical endpoints.
The general image condition should not be called a Segal condition without
specifying the instance.

For Hoapa, ask whether elementary shapes of admissible work can both
generate operational structure and characterize reusable descriptions.
Identify the arities, semantics, laws, and missing hypotheses before
asserting K is an algebra category. The contextual presentation category
may differ from the category of underlying contextual data.

The two proposed role pairs are:

```text
Free ⊣ Forget        generation / underlying contextual data
Realize ⊣ Nerve      assembly / coherent description by probes
```

Two such adjunctions can share endpoints, but their interaction is additional
data. If their right adjoints are naturally isomorphic, their left adjoints
are too; renaming a functor does not yield a distinct process. If they are
distinct, specify a comparison of descriptions, its mate, or another actual
compatibility law. Even a comparison of counits needs well-typed maps between
their different source objects.

Free/forgetful reconstruction and probe-based reconstruction may fail for
different reasons. These differences are candidate diagnostics, not
quantitative measures supplied by the counits alone. Monadicity and density
are useful representation conditions, not methodological optimality by
themselves.

A nerve can prepare a return for replay. Uptake elsewhere additionally needs
admissible transport/interpretation, realization, and preservation of the
declared use. Probes of a public interface must not silently reconstruct the
protected live record. Direction matters: a categorical nerve can retain
noninvertible arrows; passage to a homotopy type is a different operation.

### Relative cohesion — reusable alternative, not a reinstatement

A cohesive-type relationship can be relative to a base other than Set.
Its four-functor string has the shape
`Π ⊣ Disc ⊣ Γ ⊣ Codisc`, with full-faithfulness and preservation
hypotheses in addition to the adjunctions.
[Marmolejo–Menni](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.02826);
[Shulman's account](https://homotopytypetheory.org/2011/11/02/axiomatic-cohesion-in-hott/).

This suggests asking about "K cohesive over M," rather than installing
cohesion on M over a fixed base. It is only a candidate: suitable endpoints,
geometric-morphism structure, and the relevant axioms are unbuilt. Four
functors in a chain give **three** adjacent adjunctions, not merely the two
pairs above. Nor are all four required to be distinct in every model.

A useful consequence to test, rather than hide: if Allow/Require were
identified with Disc/Γ and Disc were fully faithful, the unit would be
invertible and Γ Disc would be equivalent to the identity. That assignment
could support a stable fragment, but not the old non-idempotent monad target
for that same pair. Relative cohesion is therefore not a free solution to
all the existing requirements.

The retired specimen failed because its unrestricted rules made every type
flat-modal, not because cohesive mathematics is intrinsically degenerate.
Repairing that specimen is no longer the programme; reusing suitable
cohesive ideas remains possible.

### Scale, inheritance, and loss

Local/global and generation/description are independent axes. A compatible
family of adjunctions may assemble into one total adjunction, not a second
independent process. A doctrine-level correspondence has different endpoints
from a correspondence inside a doctrine. Name these levels before invoking
mates or Beck–Chevalley.

Density would make a realization–nerve counit invertible. Where a specified
probe family fails to reconstruct an object, that counit is a candidate
location for observation-relative Anthropic loss. This is a sharpening to
test, not an exclusive reassignment of the anchor. An exact local nerve can
coexist with protected or inaccessible structure outside its modeled scope
and with losses in other transitions.

Keep actual episode distinctions, future-use adequacy, statistical
reconstruction, and leakage separate. The two-fair-bits example explains why:
publishing one bit and independently resampling the other can recover the
joint distribution while losing the actual episode's second bit. Zero KL
divergence therefore does not establish invertible reconstruction.

Stable fixed-point or mutually adequate fragments may support inheritance;
development can occur between regimes. Distinguish the several constructions
called nuclei before importing that reading.

## 1. The question and its first mathematical form

The opening question asked whether one adjunction could be followed through
different domains, theories, or levels of complexity. There is no single
standard object called a *continuous adjunction* with that meaning. The
nearest established forms are:

- parametrized or indexed adjunctions;
- adjunctions between fibrations over a common base;
- diagrams of adjunctions in a 2- or ∞-category;
- doctrinal liftings of an adjunction through added structure.

The first approximation is an indexed pair of categories over a base `P`:

```text
M, K : Pᵒᵖ → Cat

Allowₚ : Mₚ ⇄ Kₚ : Requireₚ
Allowₚ ⊣ Requireₚ
```

An arrow in `P` induces reindexing between fibres. Comparison maps then say
whether `Allow`, `Require`, their units, and their counits commute with that
change. In this setting, *continuous* means coherent under transport. The base
need not be a topological space, a line, or even an ordered set.

This already separated three operations that the first question had run
together:

1. **variation:** move between adjunctions indexed by different situations;
2. **iteration:** repeatedly apply the monad of one adjunction inside one
   fibre; and
3. **lifting:** retain an adjunction while adding richer structure to its two
   sides.

They may eventually interact, but none implies the others.

### Mathematical structure does not become physical by accumulation

The conversation next asked whether such a family could "overflow
mathematics" and become physical. Categories already organize bordisms,
spacetimes, observables, scales, and boundary conditions in physical theories,
but an adjunction supplies no empirical meaning by itself. A physical instance
needs a compatible family of interpretations connecting formal objects and
arrows to systems, operations, measurement, and prediction. Physical meaning
can be transported; it does not appear automatically at a late stage of
categorical complexity.

## 2. Why the single Hoapa pair was enlarged

The current K architecture is grounded in a concrete problem: how a community
can conduct open, collective, deliberative inquiry while preserving equal
formal standing, participant opacity, accountable traces, plurality, and
future work. The human-ready implementation is therefore the right discovery
site. Its engineering and social constraints expose distinctions that a
purely formal search would not have found.

This does **not** mean that the human case is only empirically interesting or
mathematically arbitrary. Those constraints may force distinctive and
important mathematics. If they determine a universal property, an obstruction,
or a non-trivial invariant, the human case will be mathematically privileged
*relative to the problem it solves*. What is rejected is an a priori claim
that humanity, merely as humanity, is the unique point at which the theorem
can be stated.

The same correction applies on M. Cohesive topoi are a rich landmark for
worlds with discrete, codiscrete, shape, and possibly infinitesimal structure.
They need not exhaust the forms a decipherable context may take. The generic
question asks which structures on a world and which structures on a practice
are sufficient for the relation conjectured by Hoapa.

The initial image of a progression from discrete computation, through human
Hoapa, toward richer future practices remains useful motivation, but
*complexity* should not be installed as a total order. Transitions may be
forgetful, free, conservative, interpretive, refining, or incomparable. Their
arrows have to be defined before their geometry is called a moduli space.

The existing Hoapa levels, architectural layers, and adapters are not points
of this base. They are independent axes internal to one K architecture. A
point of the proposed base would index a whole doctrine of inquiry.

## 3. The corrected generic shape

The first proposal spoke of a moduli space on each side and an adjunction
between corresponding points. The correction is that points do not themselves
form the two ends of an adjunction. A point indexes two categories or doctrines;
the adjunction connects those fibres.

A schematic form is:

```text
B_M  ←──  P  ──→  B_K
          │
          ├── M-fibre Mₚ: a kind of world or context
          ├── K-fibre Kₚ: a kind of inquiry practice
          └── Allowₚ ⊣ Requireₚ
```

`B_M` organizes possible world-structures. `B_K` organizes possible
inquiry-practices. `P` carries witnesses that particular structures correspond.
Because the fibres and their transformations are categorical or higher, these
bases are likely 2- or ∞-categorical rather than ordinary parameter sets.

### Where the correspondence comes from

`P` is load-bearing. Three readings must be distinguished:

- a hand-selected catalogue of pairs, which has little mathematical content;
- a correspondence induced independently by how a practice probes or changes
  a world; or
- the collection of pairs for which an adjunction happens to exist.

The last reading is circular: it makes the generic adjunction true by the
definition of its base. The second is the promising one. It suggests first
constructing a category- or space-valued correspondence

```text
Corr : B_Kᵒᵖ × B_M → Cat        or        Spaces
```

and taking a suitable category of elements only afterward. An object of
`Corr(K,M)` should witness an independently defined admissibility,
interpretation, or work relation between `K` and `M`, not the adjunction one
hopes eventually to prove.

This repeats at doctrine scale the discipline already recorded for
`P_shape : Mᵒᵖ × K → Spaces`: construct the mediator independently, then test
two-sided representability. `Corr` and `P_shape` are not thereby the same
object.

### Coherence is necessary, and non-constancy is content

A pointwise collection of adjunctions is only a catalogue. For an arrow
`u : p → q`, transport on M and K must compare coherently with `Allowₚ`,
`Requireₚ`, their units, and their counits. Beck–Chevalley or mate conditions
are the likely language.

Coherence alone is still cheap: a constant family satisfies it trivially. A
non-trivial generic theory must also prove non-constancy. At least one
transition must change relevant structure without reducing to an equivalence
or to the same adjunction under renaming. This is the horizontal analogue of
the project's existing non-collapse discipline.

M must not be mistaken for a metalanguage standing outside K. It becomes the
"world-to-decipher" side only if its objects and maps are independently shown
to carry the contextual structure that K practices actually encounter.

## 4. What Caramello's programme can and cannot supply

The conversation initially proposed a Caramello-style "generalized topos" as
one free object generating all the other M-examples. That is too strong and is
not a stable technical description of her programme.

For a geometric theory `T`, its classifying Grothendieck topos contains a
universal model of `T`; models of `T` in other Grothendieck topoi are obtained
through its classifying universal property. It generates the models of that
particular theory, not all topoi or all possible worlds. Conversely, every
Grothendieck topos classifies a suitable geometric theory.

This gives Hoapa two plausible but conditional resources:

- a classifying topos could be a distinguished M-fibre once a geometric theory
  of a relevant decipherable context has been earned; and
- relative topos theory, indexed categories, stacks, and relative sites could
  help organize M-fibres varying over a base.

Caramello's bridge method also shows how different sites or theories can
present a shared semantic core. That is highly relevant to Hoapa's search for
portable technical language. Morita-equivalent presentations do not,
however, construct the K-side practice or the cross-side correspondence.

## 5. Vertical recursion and horizontal change

The enlargement revealed two distinct directions of development.

Within one fibre `p`, the local adjunction induces

```text
Tₚ = Requireₚ ∘ Allowₚ.
```

Its unit tower is the present home of T3 and T_recursion: counit
non-invertibility, monad non-idempotence, and persistent non-stabilization are
fibrewise questions.

An arrow `u : p → q`, by contrast, changes the world/inquiry regime. A
Beck–Chevalley comparison and its mate ask whether reindexing commutes with
operational opening and return. If the comparison is invertible, it transports
the vertical dynamics without adding a new defect. If not, its defect may
measure a distinct horizontal loss; it is not automatically Anthropic loss.

The eventual network may interleave the two directions, but local loss need
not accumulate: non-invertible maps may compose to an equivalence. Any claim
of large-scale domination needs an accumulation theorem. Until horizontal
structure exists, T3 and T_recursion retain their current fibrewise meaning.

## 6. Anthropic loss and Care in the enlarged setting

### Anthropic loss

At each fibre, the counit

```text
εₚ : Allowₚ Requireₚ → Id(Kₚ)
```

still measures a ceiling on reconstruction. The Anthropic reading becomes
less human-centred, not less meaningful: any situated context sees other
contexts through structures compatible with its own mode of access. The
fine-tuning appearance is a generic perspectival effect.

The indexed setting asks whether this loss transports coherently and whether
the base-change mate has an additional defect. These must not be collapsed,
and neither proves persistent development or asymptotic domination.

### Care

The checked Care anchor remains the order-2 involution `(–)⊥`. The cheapest
generic extension is a family of such involutions together with coherent
transport:

```text
transport(X⊥) ≃ transport(X)⊥.
```

The banked Sessions interpretation already shows that this is a coherence
problem: it preserves duality only up to isomorphism. The stronger idea that
Care selects viable evolutionary paths remains a theorem-shaper. It needs
closure under composition and reindexing, non-triviality or a universal
property, and the still-open 2S no-leak results; defining a subcategory with
the desired name would establish none of these.

Degenerate fibres remain useful controls. They cannot serve as substantive
members of the generic family. In particular, the current unrestricted modal
signature makes every type flat-modal, so repairing or replacing that specimen
precedes any claim that Hoapa already possesses an honest non-trivial M-fibre.

## 7. Borrow–work–give-back as the content beneath the adjunction

The discussion then reversed the order of explanation. Instead of postulating
a family of adjunctions and reading logical work into it afterward, define the
work relation first.

For each correspondence point `p`, seek an independently meaningful
profunctor

```text
Workₚ : Mₚᵒᵖ × Kₚ → Spaces,
```

where `Workₚ(M,X)` is the space of admissible episodes that draw on context
`M`, perform operational work, and return an artifact or changed operational
state `X`. If there are coherent representations

```text
Workₚ(M,X)
  ≃ Map_Kₚ(Allowₚ M, X)
  ≃ Map_Mₚ(M, Requireₚ X),
```

then `Allowₚ ⊣ Requireₚ` follows. If these representations are natural in `p`,
the fibred adjunction follows.

Borrow–work–give-back is therefore epistemically prior: it names the
phenomenon and its admissibility conditions. The indexed adjunction is its
candidate universal semantics and, if representability holds, supplies its
calculus of transposition, iteration, and reuse. This is co-individuation
rather than circular definition only if `Workₚ` was defined independently.

### Return is weaker than replay

For an episode

```text
w : Allowₚ M → X,
```

the adjunction automatically supplies

```text
w♯ = Requireₚ(w) ∘ η_M : M → Requireₚ X.
```

This contextual return is free for every adjunction. It does not show that
documentation can reconstruct, resume, translate, fork, or continue the
process. Three meanings must remain separate:

- recorder-fidelity replay, already tested by the runtime recorder;
- contextual return by adjoint transposition; and
- purpose-relative replay or inheritance, still requiring observational
  adequacy, provenance, no-leak, and a stated future operation.

Nor does a completed episode create an arrow `p → q`. Context renewal must
show independently how an artifact enables a new operation while preserving
specified achievements. Renewal may eventually generate part of the base,
but its first instance must precede that general definition.

## 8. Categorical logic as the mature precedent

Categorical logic supplies a precise precedent for the borrow–work–replay
grammar. It also corrects the initially loose claim that "Gentzenian
introduction is borrowing."

A binding introduction rule governs a complete borrowing episode. For simple
implication:

```text
Γ, a : A ⊢ b : B
──────────────────
Γ ⊢ λa.b : A ⇒ B
```

Its categorical semantics is currying:

```text
C(Γ × A, B) ≃ C(Γ, Bᴬ).
```

The moments are:

1. extend `Γ` by a temporary, otherwise unspecified `A`;
2. perform work `f : Γ × A → B` under that extension;
3. transpose it to `Λf : Γ → Bᴬ`, discharging the dependency into a reusable
   rule; and
4. replay it by application, substitution, or cut.

Introduction is therefore not merely the loan: its premise opens the loan and
its conclusion returns the work in portable form. This preserves the metaphor
without falsely applying it to every introduction rule.

In dependent categorical logic, a context projection

```text
π : Γ.A → Γ
```

induces the familiar pattern

```text
Σπ ⊣ π* ⊣ Ππ.
```

Reindexing `π*` moves a judgment into the extended context and `Ππ` abstracts
back. Beck–Chevalley gives stability under substitution; β/η govern the
abstraction/application round trip; cut composes a result into later work; and
normalization removes hidden detours.

Universal introduction is especially close to Hoapa's participant discipline:
work uses an arbitrary eigenvariable whose identity cannot occur in the
undischarged hypotheses, and the result can later be instantiated anywhere.
This is a rigorous analogue of identity-neutral participation, not a proof
about sortition, unanimity, or actor-renaming.

Ordinary structural logic permits assumptions to be copied or discarded.
Linear and other substructural logics refine the picture by tracking their
use. Hoapa's banked LNL bridge is therefore a genuine nearby precedent, but it
remains concurrency-internal and is not the conjectured cross-site
adjunction.

### Hilbert at the scale of theories

Hilbertian axioms play a different role. An axiom is not normally a temporary
loan; it helps define the ambient theory `T`. Categorical logic turns such a
theory into a syntactic or classifying category:

- borrowing means reasoning relative to `T` as an inherited context;
- work is a proof or morphism in the syntactic category of `T`;
- return stabilizes the result as a theorem or derived rule rather than a new
  independent axiom; and
- replay interprets or applies that theorem in later derivations and across
  models of `T`.

Gentzen thus supplies the local scale of temporary assumptions, discharge,
and substitution; Hilbert the global scale of inherited theory and
interpretation across models. Fibred categorical logic relates them through
contexts, reindexing, adjoints, and classifying universal properties.

The resulting theorem-shaper for Hoapa is:

> Borrow by reindexing into an extended context; work vertically there;
> return by adjoint abstraction into an inheritable trace; replay by
> substitution and composition; require normalization, base-change
> coherence, observational adequacy, and no-leak for that replay to be
> legitimate.

This gives legitimacy and precise comparison points to the logical reading.
It is not evidence that Hoapa's particular `Allow ⊣ Require` exists.

## 9. Lautman, Simondon, and Grothendieck in the enlarged picture

The three anchors no longer appear as a dictionary of nouns.

- **Lautman** supplies a problematic relation that may admit several effective
  mathematical realizations. The fibres are possible realizations; the
  correspondence and its invariants express what persists through them.
- **Simondon** prevents worlds and inquiry-practices from being treated as
  completed independently. Work can transform its milieu, and context renewal
  is the concrete K phenomenon through which their co-individuation first
  becomes mathematical.
- **Grothendieck** supplies the relative method: families over a base,
  fibrations, change of base, descent, classifying objects, stacks,
  profunctors, and representability.

Their convergence proposes a method, not evidence: begin from a concrete
problem, define the family in which its solution is relative, and let a
universal property state what has actually been learned.

## 10. Resulting conjectural architecture

The durable local statement remains:

> At the human Hoapa fibre, there are independently constructed categories M
> and K and functors `Allow : M → K` and `Require : K → M` forming the
> conjectured adjunction, with non-collapse tested at that actual pair.

The enlarged meta-conjecture is:

> There is an independently defined higher correspondence of decipherable
> worlds and inquiry-practices, carrying an indexed work doctrine. Where that
> doctrine is representable on both sides, it yields coherently varying
> `Allowₚ ⊣ Requireₚ`. The human Hoapa architecture is one concretely forced
> fibre whose distinctive constraints may produce distinctive mathematics;
> it is not assumed in advance to exhaust the family.

This formulation places the pure mathematical interest in a representation
and compatibility theorem, not in a catalogue of analogous adjunctions. It
also permits failure: some world/practice pairs may have work relations that
are not representable, and that boundary would itself be a result.

## External conversation with Claude Opus

### Q

In this lecture Riehl presents a nice setting for 2-categories:
https://youtu.be/hNqlOSHOGiQ?is=MbLRuMn4Q_JxttRN

Is this a standard construction, with a name, axioms and so on? Does it have something to do with a topos, or another structure of this kind?

### A

I'll look up the lecture first to see which setting she's using.The video is Riehl's MSRI talk, joint with Verity, in the program "Reimagining the Foundations of Algebraic Topology," Tuesday, April 8, 2014. The setting she uses is an (∞,2)-category, i.e. a simplicially enriched category where the hom-spaces are quasi-categories, with a strict 2-category as a special case. Her main result is that any adjunction in a homotopy 2-category extends to a homotopy coherent adjunction in the (∞,2)-category. Algebras and descent data then come out as weighted limits.

**Yes, it has a name and axioms.** There are two layers.

- **The 2-categorical layer is "formal category theory":** doing category theory inside an arbitrary 2-category. The classic source is Street's *The formal theory of monads* (1972), where Eilenberg–Moore objects are defined by a universal property. The talk uses the Schanuel–Street result that adjunctions in a 2-category K correspond to 2-functors Adj→K, where Adj is the free adjunction.
- **The ∞-layer was axiomatized shortly after this talk as an ∞-cosmos.** The standard reference is Riehl–Verity's book *Elements of ∞-Category Theory* (CUP 2022). An ∞-cosmos has three ingredients:
  - It is a simplicially-enriched category whose hom-spaces are quasi-categories.
  - It has a specified class of maps called "isofibrations," closed under various constructions.
  - It has a terminal object, pullbacks of isofibrations, limits of towers of isofibrations, and simplicial cotensors.

  Replacing each hom-space by its homotopy category gives a quotient 2-category of "∞-categories," "∞-functors," and "∞-natural transformations" called the homotopy 2-category. Most of the theory is done there, which is why sufficiently slick classical proofs can be transported nearly word-for-word into the ∞-categorical context. Quasi-categories, complete Segal spaces, Segal categories, and 1-complicial sets all define the ∞-categories in an ∞-cosmos, and so do several models of (∞,n)-categories.

**The connection to toposes is real, but mostly historical.**

- **The name points to a topos analogue.** Riehl and Verity note that their ∞-cosmoi resemble the fibrational cosmoi of Street. Street's *Elementary cosmoi* (1974) was an early attempt at a 2-dimensional elementary topos. Weber's *Yoneda structures from 2-toposes* (2007) made this precise: it gives a 2-categorical generalisation of the notion of elementary topos and studies the Yoneda structure it produces. The key analogy is that in Cat, the copresheaf construction classifies discrete fibrations, so discrete opfibrations play the analog of monomorphisms.
- **The ∞-cosmos axioms themselves are not topos-like.** They require no classifier, no colimits and no exponentials. Instead the maps form a category of fibrant objects à la Brown. Riehl's own analogy: an ∞-cosmos resembles a model category that is enriched over Joyal's model structure on simplicial sets, restricted to its fibrant objects. It is a "universe for category theory" in the way a simplicial model category is a universe for homotopy theory.
- **The link to ∞-toposes goes the other way.** An ∞-topos is somewhere you can build an ∞-cosmos, rather than an ∞-cosmos being a kind of topos. Riehl–Verity also build ∞-cosmoi of Rezk objects. Roughly, applied to a model of an ∞-topos, these give the ∞-categories internal to it. Martini and Wolf's work on internal higher categories, including Yoneda's lemma for categories internal to an (∞,1)-topos, develops that setting directly.

For a short entry point before the book, Riehl–Verity's *Infinity category theory from scratch* (Higher Structures, 2020) sets up the ∞-cosmos axioms and the homotopy 2-category in a few dozen pages.

### Q

You seem to imply that the relation between t topos and cosmos is that of an inclusion. Is there a kind of "structured poset", or maybe a moduli space, for this kind of "general settings in which to do mathematics"?

### A

It isn't an inclusion. A topos and a cosmos sit at different categorical levels, linked by two constructions that go in opposite directions:

- **Topos → cosmos:** take the categories internal to a topos E. Street's *Cosmoi of internal categories* (1980) is about when such internal categories form a cosmos.
- **Cosmos → topos:** take the discrete objects. In Cat these are the sets. In the ∞-cosmoi of (∞,1)-categories the discrete ∞-categories are the ∞-groupoids, which form an ∞-topos.

So a topos is roughly the "discrete part" of a cosmos, and a cosmos is roughly "categories internal to" a topos. That's closer to the relation between a ring and its category of modules than to a subclass. Weber's 2-toposes are an actual subclass, but of cosmoi: they are simply cosmoses whose underlying 2-category is cartesian closed and has a duality involution.

**Is there a structured space of such settings?** No single accepted one, but there are several good partial answers, each organizing one slice.

- **Doctrines (the "logical strength" axis).** A doctrine can name a particular setting for categorical logic, e.g. categories with finite products, or geometric logic. The best known formalism takes a doctrine to be a 2-monad on Cat. The familiar fragments form a rough ladder: finite limits, regular, coherent, geometric/pretopos, topos (higher-order logic). Free completions such as exact completion or sheafification move you up the ladder as left adjoints. When the extra structure is a property rather than data (Kelly–Lack's "property-like structures"), this ladder is close to a genuine poset.
- **Dimension (the "n-topos" axis).** Locales, 1-toposes, ∞-toposes form a fairly clean line on the groupoidal side. On the directed side it is not clear whether a single notion of (n,m)-topos exists for m>1 with most of the good properties of 1-topoi. Street's fibrational cosmoi and Weber's 2-toposes are partial encouraging results. So this grid still has holes.
- **A literal moduli space, for one doctrine.** For geometric logic, every geometric theory has a classifying topos, and every Grothendieck topos classifies some geometric theory, though not canonically. Maps F→Set[T] correspond exactly to models of T in F. So the 2-category of Grothendieck toposes really is a moduli space of geometric theories up to Morita equivalence, meaning theories that share a classifying topos. Caramello's "toposes as bridges" program is built on exactly this.
- **Set-theoretic foundations.** The generic multiverse treats models of ZFC as worlds, related by "is a forcing extension of" and "is a ground model of." It even has a computed logic: Hamkins and Löwe showed that the ZFC-provable principles of forcing are exactly the modal logic S4.2. Forcing extensions are Boolean-valued models, i.e. sheaves on a complete Boolean algebra. So this multiverse sits inside the 2-category of toposes.

Logicians have also built logic-agnostic frameworks, such as Goguen–Burstall's institutions and Uemura's framework for type theories. These give a category of "logics" whose morphisms are translations.

The closest thing to your "structured poset" is the 2-category (really 3-category) of doctrines. The closest thing to a "moduli space" is the 2-category of Grothendieck toposes, but only for geometric logic. One caveat applies to any such space: it has to be built inside some ambient setting, so it can never contain all settings including itself.

### Q

What about Caramello's work?

### A

Caramello's program is the most developed answer to your "moduli space" question, but only for one doctrine: geometric logic and Grothendieck toposes.

**The core idea: a topos is a theory up to Morita equivalence.** Any geometric theory T has a classifying topos, and two theories with the same classifying topos are called Morita-equivalent. The topos is the invariant, and each theory or site is one "presentation" of it. Her "bridge" technique works like this. Take a topos-theoretic invariant, such as being Boolean, atomic, or having enough points. Express it on the site of one presentation and on the site of another. You get two different-looking statements in two theories that are secretly equivalent.

**Your structured poset exists here.** The central result of *Lattices of theories* is a duality theorem. For any geometric theory, the subtoposes of its classifying topos are in bijection with the geometric extensions of the theory, up to syntactic equivalence. Subtoposes form a lattice with a co-Heyting algebra structure, so the quotients of T get the dual structure, including a Heyting implication.

Topos-theoretic operations then become logical ones. Examples include open, closed and quasi-closed subtoposes, and the Booleanization and DeMorganization of a theory. So each theory comes with an honest structured poset of "refinements," and Morita-equivalent theories have isomorphic ones.

A version of this correspondence also appears in Johnstone's *Elephant* (Examples B4.2.8(i)). Caramello's paper gives two different proofs and develops the transfer systematically.

**Letting the base vary.** Her recent work with Zanfa, *Relative topos theory via stacks* (2021), replaces Set by an arbitrary base. A central result is an adjunction between toposes over the sheaves on a site (C,J) and C-indexed categories. It also lets one regard any Grothendieck topos as a 'petit' topos attached to a 'gros' one, answering a problem Grothendieck posed in the seventies. In your terms, the "space of settings" becomes fibred over a base setting, rather than being a single space over Set.

**Where it stops.** The program is about Grothendieck 1-toposes and geometric (first-order) logic. So it doesn't directly cover:

- elementary toposes and higher-order logic, except through Grothendieck toposes;
- ∞-toposes;
- the directed side (cosmoi, ∞-cosmoi) from the Riehl talk.

There are first steps upward. Her 2025 papers include *Sheaves on bicategories* (with Pivet) and *Double categories of sites* (with Osmond).

In the earlier picture, then: on the "doctrine ladder," Caramello takes the geometric rung and turns it into a space. The points are toposes, and the local structure at each point is the lattice of theory refinements. The higher rungs and higher dimensions don't yet have a comparable theory.

Her OUP book *Theories, Sites, Toposes* (2017) covers the duality theorem and the lattice operations in detail.

### Q

Have you mentioned comprehension categories? 
And more generally, has there been efforts to construct a framework/theory to render a sort of all-encompassing "evolution of mathematics"?

### A

No, I hadn't mentioned comprehension categories, and they belong in this picture.

**Comprehension categories**

In Jacobs's definition, a comprehension category has two parts. The first is a fibration of "types over contexts." The second is a functor sending each type A in context Γ to a map Γ.A → Γ, the projection from the extended context. This is the categorical form of context extension, and it is the standard semantics of dependent type theory. On the "doctrine ladder" from before, it is the dependent-types rung. Locally cartesian closed categories model extensional type theory there, and ∞-toposes model homotopy type theory.

They are also a small-scale version of your "structured poset" question. Type theorists invented many competing frameworks: contextual categories, categories with families, display map categories, and others. Ahrens, Lumsdaine and North (2024) used comprehension categories as a common language. They showed that almost all established notions of model embed as sub-2-categories, usually full, of the 2-category of comprehension categories. So one 2-category holds the whole zoo, with each framework as a region inside it.

The name comes from Lawvere's hyperdoctrines. There, set-builder comprehension {x | φ(x)} appears as an adjoint functor. Jacobs's D-categories make the link explicit, connecting context extension in type theory with the comprehension schema of logic. So comprehension is a meeting point of type theory, Lawvere's doctrines and fibrations.

**Frameworks for an "evolution of mathematics"**

There have been several attempts, but no accepted all-encompassing theory. They fall into four groups.

- **Cultural and historical.** Wilder's *Evolution of Mathematical Concepts* (1968) proposed studying mathematics as a human artifact and a cultural phenomenon, in anthropological terms. It had little influence on mathematical research, but some on the history and philosophy of mathematics. Related work includes Lakatos's *Proofs and Refutations*, on how concepts get stretched under counterexamples, and Kitcher's *The Nature of Mathematical Knowledge*. There was also a 1970s–90s debate over whether mathematics has Kuhnian "revolutions" (Crowe; Gillies, ed.).
- **Dialectical and categorical.** Lawvere proposed that key parts of Hegel's *Logic* can be formalized in categorical logic, notably unity of opposites and Aufhebung (sublation). Unity of opposites becomes an adjoint pair of idempotent modalities. Schreiber and others on the nLab extended this into a sequence of adjoint modalities in modal homotopy type theory. It is a formal model of how concepts refine one another, not of actual history, and it is openly speculative.
- **Mechanisms of invention.** Goguen modelled "conceptual blending" as colimits of theories. The EU project COINVENT aimed to give a formal and computational model of human concept creation on that basis. One result generated concepts from fields and Galois theory as blends of basic concepts from group theory and topology. Critics note the approach still lacks a proper semantic dimension. Automated discovery systems, from Lenat's AM (1976) to Colton's HR, attack the same question computationally.
- **Structural maps.** The "little theories" method and MMT-style theory graphs record mathematics as a network of theories joined by interpretations. Large formal libraries like Lean's mathlib are becoming de facto versions of such a map.

**The gap.** The formal work models individual moves, each as a precise operation:

- adding axioms, which corresponds to passing to a subtopos;
- blending, as a colimit;
- generalizing, as a change of doctrine;
- categorifying;
- unity of opposites, as an adjunction.

The historical work describes the dynamics, meaning which moves actually get made and why. No framework joins the two into a theory that explains the path mathematics took. Lawvere's program is the most ambitious attempt, and David Corfield's *Towards a Philosophy of Real Mathematics* (2003) is a good bridge from the philosophical side.

### Q

It seems that the 2-category structure and type dependency are two pillars of many attempts at structuring the whole of mathematics. Can we find reasons why? 
Also, what other pillars are there in those kinds of efforts?

### A

The two pillars keep appearing because they handle the two things every piece of mathematics needs: saying when two constructions count as the same, and letting objects vary over parameters. 2-categories handle sameness; type dependency handles variation. They are also secretly the same idea seen from two sides.

**Why 2-categories**

- **Settings have maps between maps.** Once your objects are whole settings (categories, toposes, theories), the maps between them (functors, interpretations) have their own comparisons (natural transformations). So a universe of settings is at least 2-dimensional, just as Cat is. In general a universe of n-dimensional things is (n+1)-dimensional, which is why the ∞-cosmos is an (∞,2)-category.
- **Sameness of settings is equivalence, not equality.** Two categories are "the same" when there is an equivalence between them, and that notion needs invertible 2-cells. Any structuralist framework that respects "only structure matters" has to track these isomorphisms one level up.
- **Adjunctions live there.** Free/forgetful, syntax/semantics, Galois connections, quantifiers: the most common relation between settings is an adjunction. It is defined by 2-cells (unit and counit), so the 2-category is its minimal home. That is exactly why Riehl can do adjunctions, monads and descent in any 2-category.
- **Logic is already 2-dimensional.** In a hyperdoctrine, contexts are objects, terms are 1-cells and entailments between formulas are 2-cells. Keeping track of proofs, not just provability, forces the extra dimension.

**Why type dependency**

- **Mathematics is full of families.** Fibers of a bundle, "for each x, a set B(x)", "let H be a subgroup of G and x ∈ G/H": ordinary mathematical language is dependently typed. Set theory hides this behind membership, while type theory and proof assistants make it explicit.
- **Grothendieck's relative point of view.** Working over a base S, with objects over S and base change, is dependency made geometric. It turned out to be the engine of modern algebraic geometry.
- **Logic falls out of it.** Lawvere showed that ∃ and ∀ are the left and right adjoints to substitution, i.e. pulling back along a change of context. Dependency plus adjunctions is essentially first-order logic.

**Why they come together**

The Grothendieck construction converts one into the other. A family of categories depending on an object of C (a fibration over C) is the same as a pseudofunctor from C^op into Cat, and that is a map into a 2-category. Comprehension categories, Street's cosmoi with their fibration classifiers, and univalent universes are all versions of this. Univalence is where the two pillars fuse: inside dependent type theory, equality of types is declared to be equivalence. Historically, Klein's Erlangen program (invariance under transformations) is the sameness side, and Grothendieck's relative viewpoint is the variation side.

**Other recurring pillars**

- **Universal properties and representability.** Arguably deeper than 2-categories themselves: the Yoneda lemma says objects are determined by their maps.
- **Duality.** Space versus algebra (Stone, Gelfand, Isbell), theories versus models (Gabriel–Ulmer, Makkai). Caramello's bridges and Lawvere's functorial semantics rest on this.
- **Locality and gluing.** Sheaves, stacks and descent: local data plus compatibility gives global data. This is what makes a topos a topos, and it is where Riehl's talk ends up.
- **Homotopy and weak equivalence.** Identifying things up to coherent higher equivalence pushes the 2 in "2-category" to ∞. Localization at weak equivalences, model categories and ∞-categories all live here.
- **Universes and classifiers.** Ω, the object classifier and univalent universes: the setting contains an object that represents its own parts. This is also where size and self-reference problems sit.
- **The proofs–programs–categories correspondence.** Curry–Howard–Lambek puts syntax, computation and semantics in one triangle, which makes constructivity and computation a structural pillar rather than a philosophical preference.
- **Enrichment and non-cartesian structure.** Changing what hom-sets are (Bénabou's cosmoi), monoidal and linear structure, string diagrams. This is the pillar for resource-sensitive and quantum settings.
- **Algebraic presentation.** Generators and relations, monads, operads, Lawvere theories: theories themselves become algebraic objects, which is what makes "doctrines" definable at all.
- **Modalities.** Cohesion, shape, flat and sharp: geometric and topological structure added as operators on a type theory (Lawvere, Schreiber, Shulman).
- **Directionality.** Groupoidal versus directed: spaces versus categories, ∞-toposes versus ∞-cosmoi, ordinary versus directed type theory.

These pillars look universal partly because most "structure all of mathematics" projects come from the category-theory and type-theory community. Set-theoretic programs organize mathematics very differently. Their pillars are membership and the cumulative hierarchy, definability, forcing, and consistency strength measured by large cardinals. Woodin's Ultimate L program and Hamkins's multiverse are the two poles there. The two traditions meet mainly through forcing-as-sheaves and through interpreting type theories in set-theoretic models.

### Q

What are the obvious obstructions to having a unified theory with all those pillars and control/measuring mechanisms for each of them? 
And can those obstructions be leveraged productively to create the efficient theory that will render the evolution of mathematics?

### A

Every serious obstruction here says one of two things: a single setting can't contain everything, or two pillars can't both hold at full strength in the same setting. The productive move, which the field already makes locally, is to stop treating these as dead ends. Stratify instead of choosing, and let the obstruction itself generate the next setting.

**The obvious obstructions**

1. **Self-reference.** A framework containing all settings must contain itself. That gives Russell and Girard paradoxes (a type of all types is inconsistent). Gödel adds that no consistent strong theory proves its own consistency, and Tarski that it cannot define its own truth. So any "total" theory is either incomplete or a hierarchy of universes with no top.
2. **Coherence explosion.** Each extra dimension multiplies the coherence data, and weak structures can't in general be strictified. Simpson showed that strict ∞-groupoids don't model all homotopy types. Explicit control of everything becomes unmanageable.
3. **Pillars conflict at full strength.** Some examples:
   - Univalence is inconsistent with excluded middle for all types, though fine for propositions.
   - Cartesian structure (copying freely) conflicts with linearity (no-cloning).
   - Directed univalence is much harder than the groupoidal kind.
   - Extensional equality makes type-checking undecidable, which clashes with computation.
   - Choice fails in most sheaf toposes.
4. **No common scale.** Each pillar has its own natural measure:
   - consistency strength (roughly linear);
   - dimension (an integer or ∞);
   - logical strength (the doctrine poset);
   - computational complexity;
   - expressivity.

   Interpretability between arbitrary theories is a messy partial order, not a scale.
5. **Invariants versus presentations.** The meaningful content is Morita-invariant, as in Caramello's toposes. Yet actual mathematical change happens in presentations: people change definitions, notation and sites.
6. **Evolution-specific limits.** "Interesting" and "important" aren't formal invariants. History is contingent, driven by applications, culture and pedagogy. A theory that could predict the next concept would already contain it, which is self-reference again.

**Turning them into mechanisms**

- **Incompleteness becomes a motor.** Turing's ordinal logics (1939) and Feferman's transfinite progressions (1962) model theory growth as iterated reflection: add "this theory is consistent," repeat along ordinals. The obstruction produces a canonical direction of development, and the hierarchy itself is the measuring device. There is also a striking empirical fact: natural theories turn out to be well-ordered by consistency strength, and nobody knows why. That is a genuine candidate "law" of mathematical evolution.
- **Conflicts become adjunctions.** This is Lawvere's unity of opposites made concrete. When two pillars clash, build a setting with two layers joined by an adjunction, each layer keeping one pillar at full strength:
  - linear/non-linear models (Benton) join the cartesian and the linear;
  - two-level type theory joins strict and weak equality;
  - cohesive modalities join the discrete and the continuous;
  - modal sub-universes can be classical inside a constructive setting.

  Every conflict on the list above is a recipe for a new stratified setting. That is a generative rule, and it resembles how mathematics has actually grown.
- **Coherence becomes synthesis.** Don't control coherence; axiomatize the invariant behavior and let the models vary. Riehl–Verity's ∞-cosmos and homotopy type theory both do this. Comparing models then becomes the measurement.
- **No common scale becomes multiple invariants.** Treat the space of settings as a 2-category with translations as maps. Each "measure" is then a functor out of it (consistency strength, dimension, decidability), and tradeoffs are coordinates rather than failures.
- **Presentation-dependence becomes a bridge.** Caramello's method turns "many presentations of one invariant" into the mechanism that transfers results. Changing presentation is how the invariant gets explored.
- **Non-definable value becomes data.** Keep "interestingness" out of the formal core and measure it empirically. Useful proxies include compression: a good definition shortens many proofs, which is measurable on a library like mathlib. Schmidhuber's "compression progress" theory of curiosity is one formal version. Dependency and citation graphs record the actual trajectory.

**What an efficient theory would look like**

It would plausibly have two layers:

- **A formal space of possible settings.** Its geometry is given by these obstructions: universe levels, stratifying adjunctions, incompatibility axes.
- **Empirical dynamics on that space.** These are fitted from data rather than derived: which moves were made, which compressed the most.

The self-reference obstruction also guarantees such a theory can never be final. Any version of it is itself a piece of mathematics, so it must be open to reflection and extension like everything else. That isn't a defect: a theory of mathematics' evolution that could close itself off would contradict the very evolution it describes.

### A

I think most of these points are right, and 3, 5 and 6 fit together into one picture. Here is my take on each.

**1. Lowering rigor.** Yes, partly. Gödel and Tarski only bite a formal system that is strong enough and talks about itself, so an informal historical account like Lakatos's or Wilder's is immune. You don't need to give up rigor everywhere, though. A better model is physics' effective theories: rigorous locally, informal in the glue between regions. The obstruction returns only for the parts you choose to formalize, and only if they try to describe themselves.

**2. Forgetting the objects.** Yes, and this is the most natural categorical move: a 2-category of theories whose objects are opaque, with only translations and interpretations visible. Each theory keeps its own coherence. There is a nice twist here: the Yoneda lemma says a surface need not be shallow. If you keep enough maps, the objects are recovered from them. So the real design choice is which maps to keep. Interpretations and Morita equivalences give a fairly coarse surface. The cost is that the reason a move happened often lives in the depth. Schemes make sense only from inside algebraic geometry.

**3. Incompatibilities as cohomology.** I think this is the strongest idea on your list. Cohomology is exactly the mathematics of "locally consistent, globally not," and its nonzero classes are content, not failures. History fits the pattern well. Failure of unique factorization gave ideals. The missing square root of −1 gave complex numbers. Noncommutativity gave noncommutative geometry. In each case the obstruction class became a new object.

There is also a precise precedent. Abramsky and collaborators used sheaves and Čech cohomology to measure contextuality, meaning the incompatibility of local observations in quantum mechanics. So "incompatibility of local perspectives measured cohomologically" is not just a metaphor. Treating the space of settings as a site with coefficients hasn't been done, though, so for now this is a program, not a theorem.

**4. Slickness means trivial evolution.** Mostly agreed: a single total scale makes evolution a line with no branching. I'd refine it slightly. A slick measure can coexist with rich dynamics. Consistency strength is almost a well-order, yet huge amounts of mathematics sit at the same level. Evolution lives in the fibers of a measure, and in the mismatch between several measures that disagree. That mismatch is exactly where point 3's cohomology would live.

**5. Absorbing the mess.** Yes, and comprehension categories show the pattern: a zoo of frameworks, then a common language in which the differences become sub-2-categories. Absorption has two features worth noting, though:

- **It is retrospective.** The unifying language comes after the zoo.
- **It creates a new level.** The unifying language becomes a pillar with its own competitors: models of ∞-categories led to ∞-cosmoi, which now sit alongside other synthetic approaches.

In your terms from 3, absorbing trivializes some obstruction classes and creates new ones one level up. That rhythm (zoo, unification, new zoo higher up) might itself be the best candidate for a law of mathematical evolution.

**6. Explanation without prediction.** I largely agree, and it also dissolves the self-reference problem. A theory that doesn't predict doesn't need to contain its successors. Two caveats:

- **Good maps suggest.** Mendeleev's table explained, but its gaps predicted elements. A digested map of mathematics will show missing bridges and empty cells whether you want it to or not. So "produces no new math" is probably unattainable; "suggests but does not produce" is realistic.
- **Thom's own example shows the cost.** Catastrophe theory was explicitly a classification of forms rather than a predictive theory, and it was heavily criticized for exactly that (Sussmann and Zahler, notably). Giving up prediction is defensible, but expect to have to defend it.

Taken together, your points sketch a coherent view: the evolution of mathematics as the cohomology of a space of settings, where unification kills some classes and creates new ones higher up. The theory's job is to digest and explain this, not to predict it. I find that more convincing than the single-scale "efficient theory" I gestured at in my last answer.

### Q

Among all those structures and pillars, does the statement that the most stable, the best candidate for a thread that links all of them, is the relative viewpoint, precisely because it is the most flexible and capable of shifting throughout the evolution described by the theory, while always remaing true, useful and generative?

### A

I think the claim is largely right, with two amendments that make it stronger rather than weaker.

**Why it's a strong candidate.** The relative viewpoint (objects over a base, with base change) shows up inside almost every pillar we listed:

- Dependent types are types *over* a context.
- Sheaves and descent are data *relative to* a cover.
- Classifiers and universes classify objects *over* a base.
- Forcing is set theory *relative to* a ground model.
- Caramello's recent program is literally relative topos theory.
- Modules over a ring give the linear, non-cartesian version.

It also survives changes of foundations, because it is a pattern (a family over a base, stable under pullback) rather than a particular structure. And it applies to itself. Your evolutionary framework can be relative to a historical base, "mathematics as of stage t," which matches your points about local rigor and explanation without prediction.

**Amendment 1: it isn't independent of sameness.** Base change is only coherent up to isomorphism: pulling back twice agrees with pulling back once only up to canonical isomorphism. So a relative viewpoint is exactly a pseudofunctor into a 2-category, which is the Grothendieck construction again. The thread is really a pair: relative plus invariant, Grothendieck plus Klein. Its generativity also leans on adjunctions, since base change has adjoints on both sides (Σ ⊣ pullback ⊣ Π), and those adjoints are the quantifiers. So "relative viewpoint" is best read as the name for this whole cluster, with relativity as its most visible face.

**Amendment 2: what generates is where it fails.** A principle that "always remains true" risks being empty, since everything is trivially relative to a point. The content lies in what does *not* behave well:

- properties that aren't stable under base change;
- local data that doesn't descend;
- sections that exist locally but not globally.

That is exactly the cohomology idea from earlier: cohomology measures the failure of the relative viewpoint to glue.

So I'd rephrase your statement like this. The relative viewpoint is the most stable thread, because it holds in every setting and moves with the settings as they change. Its failures of descent are the engine of evolution. The thread stays true; the obstructions to gluing along it are where new mathematics comes from. That keeps your "always true, useful and generative," but locates each property precisely. It is *true* as a framework, *useful* through base change and its adjoints, and *generative* through its obstruction classes.

One caution: this is my synthesis of our discussion, not an established thesis. Grothendieck's own writing on the relative point of view comes closest to endorsing it.

### Q

Now, let me first explain what I believe is going on with the anthropic principle in theoretical physics. I think this "appearance of a conspiracy on the behalf of the universe to create us humans" (which can also be extended to biological evolution conspiring to create talking primates, though not as spectacularly) is actually a sort of optical illusion due to the fact that us humans see the universe from our particular point of view, from which a lot of the universe is hidden, and what's left is precisely the part that constitutes our particular point of view. But there are no privileged points of view, and if we were somewhere else in the bigger universe we would notice the same phenomenon, only encoded with different data, the data of this other particular point of view. (This would have to do with Abramsky contextuality or AdS/CFT.) But science is too attached to the conflicting principle that there is an objective and neutral point of view on the universe (its own), that it can't really formulate this idea (which is, I concede, purely speculative).

Now, could we find the same kind of anthropic principle selectively showing a fragment of the mathematical world to a mathematical viewpoint in the ideas of the relative viewpoint of Grothendieck? And would that help in making sense of what we're discussing here?

### A

Yes. Grothendieck's framework contains a precise version of your idea, and it also offers a way out of the dilemma you describe. You don't need a view from nowhere to have objectivity: what you need is the coherent family of all views.

**Precise mathematical versions of the "perspectival illusion"**

- **Skolem's paradox.** A countable model of set theory believes it contains uncountable sets, because the bijection that would reveal them as countable lives outside it. Skolem himself concluded that set-theoretic notions like "uncountable" are relative to the viewpoint. This is your anthropic effect almost verbatim: what looks like an absolute fact is an artifact of what the viewpoint cannot see.
- **Internal logic of a topos.** An internal mathematician in a topos sees ordinary-looking mathematics, but its truths differ from ours. In some toposes every function ℝ→ℝ is continuous; in others every function is computable, or infinitesimals exist. From inside, each world's axioms feel like "just how mathematics is."
- **Set is privileged only because it's the base.** In classical topos theory, Set looks special because every Grothendieck topos is defined *over* Set. Its apparent privilege is exactly an artifact of the chosen base, and relative topos theory, including Caramello's recent work, removes it. The mathematics we practice (classical, Boolean, Set-based) is the view from one base among many.
- **The set-theoretic multiverse.** Hamkins argues that no model of set theory is privileged. Statements like CH look settled from inside a given universe but vary across the multiverse.

**How Grothendieck formalizes "no privileged view, yet something objective"**

This is where it helps with the worry you raised about science. Grothendieck's functor of points describes a scheme X through the sets X(R) for all rings R. Each R is a viewpoint, no single R sees all of X, and X *is* the coherent family of all these views. The Yoneda lemma guarantees nothing is lost this way. For toposes, the same role is played by generalized points: maps from arbitrary toposes, not just from Set.

Two further facts sharpen your picture:

- **Some worlds have no classical observers at all.** There are toposes with no points, i.e. no view from Set, yet they are perfectly coherent mathematical worlds. They are fully visible only through generalized points. So reality is more than the union of the viewpoints we happen to have, which is a strong form of your "a lot is hidden."
- **The objective fragment can be computed.** Geometric logic is exactly the part of truth that transfers along every change of viewpoint, since inverse images preserve geometric formulas. Negation, universal quantification and excluded middle are not preserved: they are perspectival. So the frontier between "objective" and "perspectival" truth is itself a theorem, not a philosophical stance.

This matches how modern physics already handles the issue, via invariance rather than a view from nowhere: general covariance, and more radically relational quantum mechanics and QBism. Abramsky's contextuality results say there are local sections without a global one. So I'd soften your claim that science can't formulate the idea: it can, but the formulations are contested, and your extension to fine-tuning goes beyond any of them.

**What it does for our discussion**

- **The evolutionary map should be a functor of points.** It should record mathematics as seen from each base (each setting, each historical stage) together with the compatibilities between views, rather than aiming at a view from nowhere. Evolution becomes change of base. That fits your preference for explanation over prediction.
- **Some apparent universality is an anthropic effect.** I noted earlier that the pillars look universal partly because most unification projects come from the category and type theory community. That is precisely a viewpoint effect.
- **An analogue of fine-tuning.** Perhaps some of the "unreasonable effectiveness" of mathematics, and the miraculous-seeming connections like moonshine or Langlands, come partly from selection. We only see the part of mathematics connected to our base, so everything we see looks connected. This is speculative, but it follows your logic.
- **It completes the earlier picture.** The anthropic illusion is mistaking a local section for a global one. The obstruction to gluing views, which we called the cohomology of the space of settings, is then both where new mathematics comes from and what the illusion hides.

**Where the analogy strains**

In physics the anthropic principle conditions on observers existing at all. Mathematics has no equivalent "we couldn't have existed otherwise." There is only the weaker selection that we can do the mathematics we can conceive and prove, which is roughly Lakoff and Núñez's embodied-mathematics thesis. So the mathematical version is a selection effect on *access*, not on *existence*. That is arguably the cleaner version of your idea anyway, since it doesn't require any conspiracy, only a base.

### Q

Regarding your last point about the absence of observers (which is contestable since mathematicians do observe mathematics, and are even more important/active in this world than observers of the universe, but that's another topic), do you think we could encode the evolution of mathematics as a fibered adjunction between on one side the mathematical worlds as/of relative structures and on the other a continuum of methodologies for a "community of (unityped) inquirers" to best approach its (Lautmanian) dialectical/metaphysical problems, where the adjunction encodes the relation as a "generator of new contexts/mathematical worlds" (the free part) and the anthropic principle (the forgetful part)? And where the methodologies can be interpreted as actual organisations for groups of humans to work together as well as completely abstract "processes" existing as duals to "worlds" in pure mathematics, completely devoid of any notion of research or communication.

### A

Fair point about observers. My claim was narrower: mathematicians' *existence* doesn't depend on which mathematics is true, the way physical observers' existence depends on physical constants. Mathematicians certainly observe, and they act on what they observe more than cosmologists do.

On your proposal: yes, I think it can be made precise. It has a well-established prototype, and its components acquire exact meanings that fit what we've been discussing.

**The prototype already exists**

Lawvere's *Adjointness in Foundations* (1969) put syntax and semantics into an adjunction: theories on one side, models on the other. Topos theory gives two sharp instances:

- **Lambek–Scott:** the free topos generated by a higher-order theory, and the internal language of a topos, form an adjunction that is even an equivalence.
- **Classifying toposes:** a geometric theory freely generates a world, its classifying topos, which contains the generic model. Models of the theory inside any world W correspond to maps from W into that classifying topos. This is essentially your "free" direction.

Your proposal generalizes "theory" to "methodology," meaning a way of approaching a problem rather than just a set of axioms. The free functor becomes the generator of new worlds, and the forgetful functor, a world reduced to what can be done from within it, becomes the anthropic principle.

**What each piece of the adjunction would mean**

- **Counit (anthropic gap).** The counit compares the world regenerated from its inhabitants' methodology with the actual world. When it isn't an isomorphism, the difference is exactly the hidden part, and this is measurable. In Vickers's *Topology via Logic*, open sets are properties affirmable by finite observation, points are worlds, and sober spaces are those where observation determines the world. Toposes without enough points are worlds that methods based on classical points cannot recover. So the anthropic illusion is "the counit looks invertible from inside."
- **Unit (conservativity).** The unit asks whether a methodology learns something new by passing through the world it generates. If it doesn't, the world is conservative over the method; if it does, the world has taught the method something.
- **Fibered structure (Beck–Chevalley).** Fibering over contexts is the relative viewpoint again. The key condition is Beck–Chevalley: generating a world and then changing context agrees with changing context and then generating. Where it fails, the order of operations matters, and that failure is a natural home for the obstruction classes we called the engine of evolution.
- **The monad (research cycles).** The adjunction induces a monad on methodologies (generate a world, then read back what it allows) and a comonad on worlds. Iterating them gives a tower, which is exactly the construction from Riehl's lecture: algebras of the monad are self-consistent methodologies, and comonadic descent is the reconstruction of worlds from local methodological data. The conversation comes full circle here. Peirce's truth, what the community of inquirers would converge on "in the long run," reads naturally as the limit of that tower.

**The two readings of "methodology"**

These need to be separated by an extra layer. Abstract processes dual to worlds exist in pure mathematics. Stone duality between logics of observation and spaces is one example; processes as coalgebras dual to algebras is another, as is Abramsky's domain theory in logical form. Human organisations are a different kind of object. I'd put a forgetful functor from organisations to processes, which keeps a group's formal shadow (what it can observe, construct, prove) and forgets its sociology. The adjunction with worlds then acts on the shadow. This structure keeps the pure-mathematics reading free of any notion of research or communication, as you want, while still letting the human reading factor through it.

If "unityped" means inquirers with no intrinsic types, there is a lovely precedent. In Scott's models of the untyped λ-calculus, types emerge as retracts (idempotents) of one universal domain. Methodologies could likewise be idempotents on an undifferentiated community, emerging from organisation rather than from differences between agents.

**Lautman and existing work**

Lautman's dialectical pairs (local/global, continuous/discrete, intrinsic/extrinsic) are exactly what Lawvere formalized as adjoint pairs. So your adjunction is itself a Lautmanian Idea: the dialectic between world and method, incarnated in every fiber. The closest existing work is Fernando Zalamea's *Synthetic Philosophy of Contemporary Mathematics*, which explicitly combines Peirce, Lautman and Grothendieck. It is worth reading as a neighbour to your project, though it stays philosophical rather than building the adjunction.

**Caveats**

- **Morphisms and direction must be fixed.** What is a map of methodologies? Maps of worlds (geometric morphisms) run contravariantly to what you might expect, so which functor is left adjoint depends on these choices.
- **The risk is staying at the level of metaphor.** The antidote is a concrete test fiber. A good candidate is geometric theories versus Grothendieck toposes, fibered over base toposes via Caramello's relative topos theory. There, every piece above (free generation, internal view, counit gap, Beck–Chevalley) is already a theorem. If your framework reproduces that case exactly and extends it, it is more than an analogy.

### Q

Putting aside this Helsinki paranthesis, my next question is: 
Can we attach a sort of metric (and we discussed how scales can be simplistic if just a map to R, or more complex, and it's the second kind I think we need) that will give us an appreciation of how well adjusted a methodology is to a mathematical world? What would this metric look like?

### A

Yes, and the natural answer is not a number but a *profile*. Fit should be measured by how far the adjunction between methods and worlds is from being an equivalence at a given pair (M, W), recorded in a structured value space. Lawvere already showed how to make "metric" mean exactly this.

**The right notion of metric: enrichment**

Lawvere (1973) observed that a metric space is a category whose hom-values are distances in [0, ∞]. The triangle inequality is then just composition: going from A to B and then B to C costs at least going from A to C. His metrics are also asymmetric, which we want, since how well M fits W is not how well W fits M.

The point for your question is that [0, ∞] can be replaced by any richer value space V: a lattice of truth values, ordinals, a category, a space. Choosing V *is* choosing how complex the scale is. A real-valued scale is the special case V = [0, ∞]. It can also be recovered afterwards as a shadow of the richer value, for example via Leinster's *magnitude*, which extracts a number from an enriched category much as Euler characteristic does from a space.

Relativity comes for free. In a given context, V can be that context's own truth values, so the fit is judged from inside the base rather than from nowhere.

**What the profile should record**

I'd say at least three axes, one for each part of the adjunction, since they pull against each other:

- **Fidelity (the counit).** How much of W can M reconstruct? Model theory gives a concrete graded version. Two structures are closer the longer the Ehrenfeucht–Fraïssé game for M's logic lasts before they are distinguished. This yields an ordinal-valued (ultra)metric relative to the logic, with Scott rank as the complexity of pinning W down.
- **Economy (cost).** How cheaply does M reach W's truths: proof length, compactness, a complete proof system? Descriptive complexity makes this exact, since logics correspond to computational resources. For instance, fixed-point logic captures polynomial time on ordered structures, and whether some logic captures it without the ordering is a famous open problem, a clean example of a mismatch between method and world.
- **Generativity (the unit).** How much does M produce beyond W: new worlds, new questions? A method perfectly fitted to one world (the complete theory of W) generates nothing. This axis keeps the metric from rewarding overfitting.

A classic triad shows the trade-off:

| Logic | Fidelity on countable structures | Economy |
|---|---|---|
| First-order | Low: nonstandard models exist | High: compactness, complete proof system |
| Second-order | Maximal: categorical descriptions | Low: no complete proof system |
| L_{ω₁ω} | Countable structures pinned down by Scott sentences | Keeps an infinitary completeness theorem, loses compactness |

Lindström's theorem says you cannot maximize both columns. So "well adjusted" should mean lying on the Pareto frontier of these axes for a given world, not scoring highest on a single scale.

**Two more layers: resolution and context**

- **Resolution.** Vary the method's resolving power (EF rounds, quantifier depth, proof length) and record what becomes distinguishable at each level. The result is a barcode, as in persistent homology, rather than a single value. It shows *at what depth* a method starts to see a world.
- **Context.** Fit varies across contexts, such as ground models or historical stages. So the full metric is a family of local fit values, varying compatibly under change of base, like a sheaf. When local fits don't assemble into a global one, the obstruction class records why. This is the same cohomological picture as before, now applied to fit rather than to worlds.

**Summary**

Put together, the "metric" is:

1. an asymmetric, enriched distance (its triangle inequality = composition of translations between methods and worlds),
2. valued in profiles of fidelity, economy and generativity,
3. graded by resolution into barcodes,
4. varying over contexts like a sheaf, with obstruction classes where local fits fail to glue.

A single number is available at the end as a decategorification, but it is a shadow.

One consequence fits the earlier discussion. Evolution would then favor methods that sit well on the frontier *across many worlds*, rather than methods perfectly adjusted to one world. That again makes the useful mismatches, not perfect fit, the engine of development.

### Q

You mentioned Scott unityped lambda calculus in a previous answer. Is there a chance that this, or more refined related versions of it, viewed as an organisation/methodology, would be a distinguished point in the "moduli space of organisations" given by the metric?

### A

Yes, I think it would be distinguished, but in a specific way: not as the best-adjusted point but as a universal and singular one. It is a corner of the space from which the other points can be obtained, and where the self-reference obstruction lives in its purest form.

**Why it's universal**

- **Everything is a retract of it.** In Scott's models, a domain D is isomorphic to its own function space [D → D]. Types appear as retracts of D, i.e. idempotent maps on it. Scott's universal domain Pω makes this precise: every countably based domain is a retract of it.
- **Typed theories come out by splitting idempotents.** Scott and Lambek showed that the untyped λ-calculus corresponds to a reflexive object. Splitting its idempotents (the Karoubi envelope) yields a cartesian closed category, i.e. a typed world.

So a conjecture in your language would be: the space of methodologies is, at least in part, the idempotent completion of the untyped organisation. Methodologies are stable roles (idempotents) that emerge within one undifferentiated community. In metric terms it is a kind of centre: every method embeds into it at no loss of fidelity.

**Why it's singular**

Lawvere's fixed-point theorem says: if an object D maps onto its own function space, every endomap of D has a fixed point. This single theorem unifies Cantor, Russell, Gödel, Tarski and the halting problem. So the untyped point is exactly where diagonalization is unobstructed. Negation has fixed points, so it cannot consistently host classical truth values. Scott's resolution is partiality: an undefined element ⊥, continuity, and computations that may not terminate.

In the fit profile, then, it sits at a vertex: maximal generativity and simulation power, minimal guarantees and economy. It is distinguished the way a singular point is distinguished (to borrow Thom again), not the way an optimum is. Typing, meaning splitting off idempotents, is what "resolves" the singularity into well-behaved regions, though that last phrase is a metaphor, not a theorem.

**Refined versions that fit your framework even better**

- **Realizability as the free functor.** A partial combinatory algebra, an untyped "organisation" of computing agents, freely generates a whole world: its realizability topos. Kleene's first algebra (computable functions acting on numbers) generates Hyland's effective topos. This is your methodology → world direction as an established construction.
- **An existing moduli space.** Longley's applicative morphisms make partial combinatory algebras into a structured category, literally a space of untyped organisations with maps between them. Every such algebra can simulate the partial recursive functions, so Kleene's algebra plays roughly the role of a base point. I'd want to check the exact initiality statement before relying on it.
- **Types emerging from interaction.** In Girard's ludics and in Krivine's realizability, a type is not given in advance. It is defined as what passes a family of tests, closed under a double "orthogonality." That closure is a Galois connection between agents and tests. So the method ↔ world adjunction reappears *inside* the untyped organisation: a methodology is characterized by what it successfully interacts with. For a community of inquirers, this may be the most faithful formal image.
- **Dialogue as organisation.** Game semantics (Abramsky; Hyland–Ong) models computation as dialogue between players. It is the natural setting if you want the organisation's communication structure, not just its outputs.

**What this suggests for human organisations**

The analogy predicts something, cautiously. An untyped organisation, with no fixed roles, is maximally flexible and generative, but it is exposed to the untyped pathologies: non-termination (endless open-ended processes) and self-referential loops. Stable roles emerge as idempotents, patterns that reproduce themselves when applied again. Well-functioning organisations would then sit near the untyped point, able to form new roles, without collapsing into it. That mirrors the conclusion about the fit metric: the productive region is near the singularity, not at it.

### Q

I'm interested in the role of ⊥.
What would it be related to in the organisations/methodologies (looking at the shadow, not the organisation directly)?

### A

In Scott's domains, ⊥ does three jobs: it means "no information yet," it is where every approximation starts, and it is where self-reference gets parked. Each of these has a clean shadow in a methodology.

**1. The starting point of inquiry.** Kleene's fixed-point theorem builds the solution of a recursive problem by starting from ⊥ and applying the process repeatedly, taking the limit of the results. The answer obtained is the *least* fixed point: the minimal commitment consistent with the problem. In the shadow, ⊥ is the state of a problem before any partial answer. A methodology's output on a problem is what it reaches by iterating from ignorance, with nothing assumed beyond what the iteration forces.

**2. Where paradox is parked honestly.** This connects to Lawvere's theorem from before: in the untyped world every map has a fixed point, including negation, and ⊥ is that fixed point (not ⊥ = ⊥). Kripke's theory of truth (1975) uses exactly this construction. Build truth values upward from "undefined": grounded sentences acquire values, and the Liar stays undefined forever. So ⊥ is not a failure: it is a stable, principled status for questions that the methodology's own reflexivity makes unresolvable. Independence results in set theory play this role for a formal methodology: a proof that CH stays at ⊥ relative to ZFC. Evolution then happens by changing the methodology (new axioms, new logics) so that the question can leave ⊥.

**3. What cannot be observed from inside.** In the Scott topology, open sets are properties that can be confirmed in finite time. ⊥ has none of them, so you can confirm an answer when it arrives but never confirm that none will come. This is the halting problem in its simplest form. In the shadow, a methodology cannot tell "working slowly" from "stuck forever" from within. That is the anthropic theme again: from inside, ⊥ always looks like "not yet." Deadlines and timeouts are an external change of methodology (adding a clock) that forcibly turns ⊥ into a decision.

**4. The test of how a methodology combines sub-inquiries.** A strict process needs all its inputs: if any sub-inquiry is at ⊥, the whole is at ⊥. A lazy process can produce output while some inputs are still undefined. The deepest example is Plotkin's "parallel or": return true as soon as either input is true, even if the other never terminates. Plotkin showed that no purely sequential program can compute it. So sequential and parallel organisation are distinguished precisely by their behaviour at ⊥. In the shadow, the question is whether the structure can route around a stuck subproblem, or whether one stalled branch blocks everything.

**5. Monotonicity, and what it leaves out.** Domains only accumulate information: moving up from ⊥ never retracts anything. That captures cumulative progress but not refutation or revision, Lakatos's actual dynamics. For those you need a top element ⊤ meaning "conflicting information," as in Belnap's four-valued logic with values ⊥, true, false and ⊤. The distinction matters in the shadow:

- ⊥: we don't know yet;
- ⊤: we have contradictory results;
- absurdity (logic's ⊥, the empty type): we know there is no answer.

These three are easy to conflate. Much methodological progress consists of separating them, for example turning "open" into either "impossible" or "independent."

In the picture we've been building, then, ⊥ is the base point of the space of states of an inquiry. Distance from ⊥ measures information gained, the least fixed point is a methodology's honest answer, and permanent ⊥-ness relative to a methodology is exactly the pressure that drives mathematics to change methodology.

### Q

Could it be linked to relations between agents?
If so, what type of relation between agents in the organisation could it model/reflect/talk about...?

### A

Yes. In fact ⊥ is fundamentally relational, and that is the key to your question.

In operational semantics, the information order is not intrinsic. Two processes are compared by testing them against every possible *context*, meaning every other agent that might interact with them. M is below N if, in every context where M produces a response, N does too. ⊥ is then the least element: the process from which no partner can ever obtain anything. So ⊥ doesn't describe an agent alone; it describes a relation in which one side receives nothing. What makes it rich is that there are several distinct reasons a partner can receive nothing, and each corresponds to a different relation between agents:

**1. Observation: who can tell whom apart.** Two agents are equivalent when no third agent can distinguish them by interacting with them. ⊥ is the agent that every observer finds identical to silence. This is the relation of *mutual accessibility*: what my partners can get from me.

**2. Dependence: waiting on someone.** In concurrent processes, a non-response can come from divergence (endless internal work) or from deadlock (a cycle of agents each waiting for another). They look identical from outside, since both are ⊥, but they come from different relations. Divergence is a property of one agent; deadlock is a property of a cycle in the "waits-for" graph. An organisation's ⊥-states can be diagnosed by asking which of these it is.

**3. Grounding: circular deference.** Take Kripke's construction to a network of agents, where each claim is accepted because someone else accepts it. The least fixed point keeps only claims that trace back to independent evidence; circular deference ("I believe it because you do, and you because I do") stays at ⊥. The greatest fixed point instead accepts any self-sustaining cycle, which is the logic of coinduction and bisimulation. So the choice between least and greatest fixed point is a choice of epistemic policy: skeptical grounding versus self-sustaining consensus. An echo chamber is a greatest fixed point that the least fixed point would leave at ⊥.

**4. Role and duality: speaker and listener.** In the continuation reading of logic, a proof of A → ⊥ is something that *consumes* an A: a listener, tester or critic for A. Negation then swaps the roles of speaker and listener. In Girard's ludics this becomes the definition of a role: a type is whatever passes all the tests passed by its testers. Ludics also separates two endings that ordinary ⊥ conflates:
- the *daimon*, where one side concedes and the interaction ends by agreement;
- divergence, where the dialogue never ends.

For a community of inquirers, that is the difference between a debate closed by mutual recognition and one where the parties talk past each other forever.

**5. Redundancy: routing around silence.** Plotkin's "parallel or" answers as soon as either of two sub-inquiries answers, even if the other is stuck forever. Purely sequential organisation cannot do this. The relation involved is independence between agents working on the same question. It is what lets an organisation survive a member at ⊥.

**6. Blame versus silence.** Programming languages distinguish ⊥ (non-response) from an *error* (an observable failure). Errors can be attributed: Wadler and Findler's blame calculus assigns responsibility to whichever party broke the contract between typed and untyped code. ⊥ cannot be attributed, because it is never observed. That is a real structural difference in the shadow of an organisation: failures that can be traced to a relation, versus silences that cannot.

So ⊥ in the shadow is the undifferentiated appearance of several different relational breakdowns: waiting, cycling, talking past each other, depending on a single path, or plain silence. A mature methodology refines ⊥ into these distinct cases, which is the relational counterpart of what I said last time about distinguishing "unknown," "contradictory" and "impossible." Much of the practical craft of organising inquiry is exactly this refinement: turning an unobservable ⊥ into a diagnosable relation.

### Q

Is there a sense in which this device can be turned into "care between generations of agents"? Where the context built by generation 1 is the food of generation 2 which in turn modify context for generation 3 (note that each generation receives by freely feeding from context, without direct "control" from the previous generation).

### A

Yes, and it fits the device closely. Care between generations becomes a property of the context one leaves behind, not of any control over successors. There are precise formal counterparts for both the transmission mechanism and what "care" would mean.

**The shape: a context comonad**

Computation that reads from a surrounding context is modelled by a *comonad* (Uustalu and Vene's "comonadic notions of computation"). Each process reads the context it finds and produces something that becomes part of the next context. The canonical example is a cellular automaton like Conway's Life: each generation is computed purely by local rules reading the previous state. No generation controls the next; it only leaves a board.

This is the comonad on worlds that our earlier adjunction already produced. Generational transmission is just composing context-reading processes one after another. Outside computing, the same structure has names: *stigmergy* (coordination through modifications of a shared environment, as with termites) and *niche construction* (organisms reshape the environment that selects their descendants). The "cumulative ratchet" of human culture is the same idea.

**What care would mean, precisely**

- **Hand down sound approximations.** Recall the fixed-point picture: a generation's answer is reached by iterating its process from a starting state. If it starts from the inherited context c, rather than from ⊥, and c is a sound underapproximation (its process only extends c, never contradicts it), iteration converges to the least answer above c. Nothing needs to be undone. So care means passing on only what is grounded and marking the rest honestly as ⊥. The harm is not ⊥; it is *false fullness*: gaps filled with guesses, or circular consensus (the greatest fixed point from last time) passed off as grounded. That forces the next generation to demolish before building.
- **Be productive: never leave successors waiting forever.** For infinite structures such as streams, a definition is *productive* if every request for the next piece is eventually met. Guarded recursion formalizes this with a "later" modality, a type for what will be available at the next step, and guarded definitions are guaranteed never to get stuck at ⊥. Care here means that whatever you hand on can always yield its next piece to whoever asks.
- **Be lazy in the good sense.** Lazy evaluation leaves structure unevaluated but evaluable, as *thunks*, and the consumer decides what to force. With call-by-need, work done once is shared by everyone after. This is freedom without control: the next generation isn't made to compute what it doesn't need, yet can obtain anything it does need.
- **Be portable across methods.** Since successors feed freely, they may use a different methodology. What survives is invariant content: results true in many presentations, stable under change of base. Caramello's multiple presentations and Kennedy's "formalism freeness" are exactly the properties that make a context digestible by methods you can't anticipate.

**⊥ as the gift**

In this light, well-marked ⊥'s are the most generous part of an inheritance. They are open questions posed so that a future method can start iterating on them. Hilbert's problems, the Weil conjectures and Grothendieck's *Esquisse d'un programme* are handed-down ⊥'s with structure: explicit space left for successors, with enough surrounding context to make them solvable.

**The limit, and how care handles it**

The monotone picture assumes inheritances only ever need extending. Real transmission sometimes requires revision. Here care means keeping the *grounding* visible: record why each item is believed, so successors can retract selectively rather than wholesale. Doyle's truth-maintenance systems did exactly this, storing justifications alongside conclusions. Provenance is what makes an inheritance revisable without being destroyed.

In short, care between generations means passing on a context that is sound where it commits, honest where it is ⊥, always productive, lazily available and portable across methods, with its grounding recorded so it can be revised. Every clause has a formal counterpart, and none requires the earlier generation to control the later one.

### Q

Do you see a place for the mathematics of 'sketch of a program' of Grothendieck, somewhere in the framework we've been sketching in this conversation?

### A

Yes. The *Esquisse d'un programme* (1984) may be the single best worked example of the framework, because almost every theme we've discussed has a precise counterpart in it.

**1. Anabelian geometry: a world reconstructed from its relations.** Grothendieck conjectured that certain curves (hyperbolic curves over number fields) are determined by their étale fundamental group together with the Galois action on it. The fundamental group records loops and paths: pure relational data. The conjecture says this "surface" recovers the whole object, the Yoneda-style point from your question about forgetting the objects. It was later proved for hyperbolic curves (Nakamura, Tamagawa, Mochizuki). It is also the relative viewpoint in its purest form: the object is known only through its symmetries as seen from a base field.

**2. Dessins d'enfants: a humble method with full fidelity.** Belyi's theorem implies that every algebraic curve defined over the algebraic numbers can be presented by a *dessin*, a graph drawn on a surface. Grothendieck was struck that the absolute Galois group Gal(Q̄/Q), the central object of arithmetic, acts faithfully on these drawings. In our terms:

- The methodology is childlike combinatorics, close to the untyped point: just drawing.
- The world is arithmetic.
- The counit is as good as it gets: nothing about the curves over Q̄ is invisible to the drawings.

Yet the Galois action on them remains deeply mysterious. So full fidelity does not mean transparency: the drawing method sees everything, but we cannot yet read what it sees. This is the anthropic theme in reverse: the hidden structure is present in the viewpoint, just not decoded.

**3. The Teichmüller tower and GT: symmetries of a method versus symmetries of a world.** Grothendieck proposed studying all moduli spaces of curves together, as a tower linked by gluing maps. In his "Lego" picture, the whole tower is generated by its pieces of dimension at most two plus the rules for assembling them. This is free generation plus descent: a world built from small generators and gluing data. Today it is seen as an operad, which puts it squarely in the algebraic-presentation pillar.

Drinfeld then defined the Grothendieck–Teichmüller group GT, the symmetry group of this gluing structure (related to braids and associators). The Galois group embeds into it (Drinfeld, Ihara), and whether the embedding is an isomorphism is a famous open problem. In our framework this has a clean reading:

- GT is the symmetry group of a *methodology*: the way surfaces are glued.
- Gal(Q̄/Q) is the symmetry group of a *world*: arithmetic.
- "Is Gal = GT?" asks whether method and world have the same symmetries.

That is exactly the fit question, and it is precisely one of the structured ⊥'s we discussed as a gift to later generations.

**4. Tame topology: an explicit critique of fit.** In the same text, Grothendieck argued that general topology, built on set theory, is badly adjusted to geometry. It admits pathologies, like space-filling curves, that geometry never needs. He called for a "tame topology" fitted to its objects. This is your fit metric applied by Grothendieck himself: a methodology with too much generality and poor economy for its world.

The evolutionary sequel is striking. Model theorists realized much of it as o-minimality (van den Dries's *Tame Topology and O-minimal Structures*, 1998). Pila and Wilkie's counting theorem then turned that model-theoretic method into a tool for arithmetic, contributing to results on the André–Oort conjecture. A change of methodology, proposed as a critique of fit and carried out by model theorists, fed back into number theory. That is one full evolutionary cycle, and it would be a good example for the Helsinki audience too.

**5. The text itself as care between generations.** The *Esquisse* is not a paper of theorems. It is a context left for others to feed on freely: marked open questions, suggested generators, frank judgments of fit. Drinfeld, Ihara, Schneps, Mochizuki, van den Dries and others each took from it without direction from Grothendieck. Its companion, *Pursuing Stacks* (1983), plays the same role for higher categories: it proposed the homotopy hypothesis and the program of modelling ∞-groupoids. That line of work runs through Joyal, Cisinski and others toward the ∞-categorical world where Riehl and Verity work. So the lecture that started this conversation sits downstream of Grothendieck's late sketches.

So the *Esquisse* would have a place in the framework as its main case study. It has worlds reconstructed from relations, a method with total but opaque fidelity, a symmetry-of-method versus symmetry-of-world question left open as a gift, and an explicit fit critique that was resolved by a change of methodology in another field.

### Q 

On a side note, something that surfaced in my work around those ideas is that the questions of origins may be ill-conceived, because they often try to explain how a "thing" came into existence, when the more primordial question should always be "how do new dimensions enter a context?" Because it's the dimensions that "then" "control" the emergence of new things in the given context. 
Does that make sense to you? 
Do you see how it could be articulated in the framework we started to sketch?

### A

Yes, it makes sense to me, and mathematics supports it well. Many "origin" puzzles about mathematical objects vanish once you ask which dimension entered the context. The complex number i looks mysterious as a thing. It becomes obvious once the number line gains a second axis, i.e. rotation enters arithmetic. After that, i is just the point at a quarter turn. The historical struggle was about admitting the dimension, not about finding the object.

There is one refinement I'd suggest, which the framework makes precise: sometimes a thing that doesn't fit forces the dimension in. So the order is not always "dimensions first." But I agree that explanation should run through dimensions.

**How the framework articulates it**

1. **Context extension is the primitive.** This is exactly what comprehension categories encode, so your question from earlier returns here. In dependent type theory the basic operation is extending a context, from Γ to Γ, x : A. Things are then terms or sections living in some context. Asking "where does this term come from?" is the wrong question. The right one is "which extension of the context made it expressible?" Comprehension is literally the operation by which a new dimension enters a context.

2. **Free generation adds dimensions, not things.** Adjoining a variable to a ring, R → R[x], adds a free dimension, and all the new elements follow from it. Our method → world adjunction works the same way. A theory's classifying topos contains a generic model, which is a new "direction" in which every particular model is a specialization. Forcing is the set-theoretic version: a generic filter adds one new coordinate to the universe. Each of its finite pieces is already decided by the old model, yet as a whole it is new, and all the new sets are built from it.

3. **Dimensions enter as ⊥ and get filled.** A newly adjoined dimension is initially all ⊥: a coordinate with no information yet. The history of an inquiry after a new dimension is the progressive filling of that coordinate. Your principle and the ⊥ picture are two phases of the same process.

4. **Cohomology classifies how dimensions can enter.** This is, I think, the strongest mathematical support for your idea. In algebra, the ways of extending a context by a new layer are classified by cohomology: group extensions by H², torsors by H¹, gerbes one level up. In deformation theory there is a clean split. H¹ gives the possible new directions (first-order deformations), and H² gives the obstructions to following them. So the obstruction classes we called "the engine of evolution" do more than block. They parametrize the possible new dimensions. An obstruction is a dimension trying to enter.

5. **Thom, once more.** Catastrophe theory separates state space from control space. Forms emerge and change as control parameters vary. The *universal unfolding* of a singularity adds exactly the control dimensions needed to make the situation stable. That is your principle almost verbatim: the dimensions govern which things can appear, and the right number of dimensions is dictated by the singularity itself.

**Consequences for the evolution map**

- **The map should track dimensions.** Its basic events are context extensions (a new axis, a new type, a new parameter, a new level of sameness), not new objects. Categorification is the textbook case: equality gains a dimension and becomes isomorphism. Homotopy theory adds a whole tower of them.
- **The table's own axes evolve.** In Deleuzian terms, a blank cell awaits a thing within the current axes. The bigger events are when the table gains a new axis, as the periodic table gained the f-block. Your principle says these axis-events are the primary history, and the filling of cells is secondary.
- **Anomalies are the exception that confirms the rule.** Sometimes an anomalous thing appears first: the Monster group before moonshine, irrational magnitudes before the real line. But it counts as anomalous precisely because it is not a section of the current context. Its existence is an obstruction, and resolving it means admitting a new dimension. So "things first" cases are dimensions arriving in disguise.
- **Old things change identity.** When a dimension enters, existing objects gain new relations. Reals acquire complex conjugates; equalities become paths with their own structure. The origin question tends to hide this retroactive effect, while the dimensional question makes it central.

So I'd phrase your principle in the framework like this: evolution is a sequence of context extensions, classified by cohomology and forced by obstructions. Things are sections that become available once the context has the right dimensions.

### Q

I'm guessing this "dimension over element" when considering emergence of novelty is an effect of the "synthetic philosophy of contemporary mathematics" to use zalamea's words, and reference Lautman's two kinds of decompositions. Can this be made rigorous/Has it been made rigorous?

### A

Yes, I think that's the right lineage. It can be made rigorous in several precise ways, even though the general thesis about novelty hasn't been stated as a theorem by anyone I know of.

One caveat first. I couldn't confirm which passage of Lautman you mean. I'll read his two decompositions as decomposition into *elements* (a number into primes, a polyhedron into simplices) versus *dimensional* decomposition. The latter is his own example from function theory: a function in Hilbert space is analysed into its coordinates along an orthogonal system. Correct me if you had a different pair in mind.

**Where it is already rigorous**

1. **Duality exchanges elements and dimensions.** Gelfand duality says the points of a compact space are the characters of its algebra of functions. Pontryagin duality says a function on a group decomposes along characters, which are its "dimensions." Stone duality does the same for logic: points are ultrafilters, and dimensions are propositions. Lawvere's reading of Isbell duality, in *Space and Quantity*, generalizes all three: every setting has a "space" side (elements, generalized points) and a "quantity" side (coordinates, observables), and the two are adjoint. Your thesis can then be stated sharply: in synthetic mathematics, the quantity side is taken as primary. Grothendieck's schemes do exactly this, defining a space through its ring of functions rather than its points.

2. **Context first, elements second: type theory.** In categorical semantics of type theory, contexts and their extensions are primitive, and elements are terms or sections relative to a context. Cubical type theory makes your wording literal. It has a separate sort of *dimension variables*, and extending a context by a new dimension i is a basic rule. A path is simply a term in a context with one extra dimension. So "how does a new dimension enter a context?" is a precise operation there, with its own rules for faces and degeneracies.

3. **Dimensions control models: model theory.** This is probably the strongest theorem-level support.
   - For uncountably categorical theories (Morley, Baldwin–Lachlan), every model is determined by a single *dimension*, the size of a basis of a strongly minimal set. The models themselves are then generated by it.
   - Shelah's Main Gap generalizes this. Either a theory is "classifiable," with models determined by dimension-like invariants attached to trees of regular types, or it has the maximum possible number of models and no such description exists.

   So the Main Gap is a sharp dividing line between worlds where dimensions govern the emergence of things and worlds where they don't. That is a good dictionary entry for the Helsinki conversation as well.

4. **How dimensions enter is classified: cohomology.** As discussed last time, extensions and deformations are classified by H¹ and H². This is the rigorous theory of the *entry* of dimensions, not just their presence.

**A candidate formal statement of your thesis**

In algebraic settings there is a canonical factorization. Every map of commutative rings R → S factors as R → R[x_s] → S. The first step freely adjoins dimensions (one variable per element of S); the second specializes by imposing relations. Every new structure is a quotient of a free one.

Your principle can be stated as: every novelty event in the evolution map factors as *adjoin free dimensions, then specialize*, and the explanatory weight lies in the first factor. The generic model in a classifying topos, and the generic filter in forcing, are both instances of this first factor. The open task is to show that the events of the evolution map admit such a factorization with some uniqueness, making it a genuine factorization system, rather than just an illustration.

**What remains philosophical**

- The priority claim, that dimensions *explain* things rather than merely present them, is not a theorem. Duality says both sides carry the same information. So the priority must come from the evolution map: dimensions are what change first, or what the history turns on.
- Zalamea's contrast between analytic and synthetic philosophy is itself a claim about which side of Isbell duality a philosophy privileges. Elementwise set-theoretic foundations favour the space side; sheaf- and Grothendieck-style mathematics favours the quantity side.

So your observation can plausibly be read as the synthetic philosophy's choice of side made explicit. That choice is well grounded in several rigorous results, but not yet a theorem about mathematics as a whole.

### Q

Regarding the issue of having a whole lot of theories "under the same roof" (comprehension, relative toposes, 2-categories, etc.), that would be the "thread of evolution as nice settings", could we use a strategy of stating specs and anti-specs, to delineate what theories must have and must not have in order to be regarded as useful/valid? 
Is this already a common practice in mathematics?

### A

Yes. It is a common practice in mathematics, though usually implicit, and some of its most successful episodes are exactly spec-first. Negative specs matter just as much, and the most useful form they take is the no-go theorem, which says certain combinations of specs are impossible.

**Specs first, construction later**

- **Eilenberg–Steenrod (1945).** They listed axioms any homology theory must satisfy and proved these determine it on nice spaces. The history then did something instructive: dropping one spec, the dimension axiom, produced "extraordinary" theories such as K-theory and cobordism. A spec used as a switch, not just a filter.
- **Weil cohomology.** Grothendieck and others listed what a cohomology theory must satisfy to prove the Weil conjectures before any such theory existed. Étale cohomology was built to meet the spec. The standard conjectures are the unmet remainder.
- **Characterization theorems.** Giraud's theorem characterizes Grothendieck toposes by a short list of exactness properties, and Lurie proved an ∞-version. Lindström's theorem characterizes first-order logic by compactness plus Löwenheim–Skolem. Such theorems turn a spec list into an identity card.
- **Unicity of higher categories.** Toën (2005) and Barwick–Schommer-Pries gave axioms that any theory of (∞,1)- or (∞,n)-categories must satisfy. They proved that all theories meeting them are equivalent. This is the most direct example of putting many theories "under one roof": the roof is a spec list, and the theorem says the roof has only one room.
- **Litmus tests.** A proposed definition of weak n-category is expected to satisfy the homotopy hypothesis from *Pursuing Stacks*. Leinster's *Survey of Definitions of n-Category* compares definitions this way. The comprehension-category paper by Ahrens, Lumsdaine and North is the same practice for type-theory semantics.
- **Type theory makes it explicit.** Designers list metatheoretic properties: consistency, canonicity, normalization, decidable type-checking, univalence. Cubical type theory was built precisely to get univalence and canonicity together.

**Anti-specs**

- **No-go theorems** are the rigorous anti-specs. They prohibit combinations rather than single properties. Lindström (no logic stronger than first-order with both compactness and Löwenheim–Skolem), Girard's paradox (no type of all types), univalence versus uniqueness of identity proofs, and Arrow's theorem in social choice are all of this form.
- **Pathology exclusion** is the informal version. Grothendieck's tame topology excludes space-filling curves. Lakatos called the historical practice "monster-barring," with the warning that it can also block genuine discoveries.
- **Grothendieck's principle** is often summarized as: better a nice category with nasty objects than a nasty category with nice objects. It is an anti-spec about where to place specs, on the setting, not on the objects. That is your dimension-over-element point again.

**A draft for our framework**

*Specs* a "nice setting" should have:
- a relative viewpoint: base change with adjoints on both sides;
- invariance under the appropriate equivalence;
- descent (local data glue);
- presentation independence, meaning Morita invariants are recognizable;
- stratified universes;
- comparison functors to other settings, i.e. bridges.

*Anti-specs* it should not have:
- a privileged base or view from nowhere;
- unstratified self-containment (type : type);
- strictness that isn't invariant;
- a single real-valued scale of value;
- conflating ⊥ (unknown), ⊤ (contradictory) and absurdity.

**Two cautions**

- **Validity is not usefulness.** A spec list can test validity (consistency, coherence). Usefulness is the fit profile, which depends on the world being studied. So the list should be layered: hard specs for validity, and soft specs meaning "good on the frontier" for usefulness.
- **Specs evolve too.** Eilenberg–Steenrod shows that dropping a spec can open a new region. By your own principle, a new dimension entering changes the spec list. So spec lists should themselves be versioned and relative to a context. The evolution map would then also record the history of specs, and the no-go theorems would chart its boundaries.

Taking your separations in turn:

**Reality vs. organisation → functoriality.** This is Leinster's sharpest contribution to your thesis. The Baez–Fritz–Leinster theorem shows Shannon entropy is the only notion of information loss that is functorial, convex-linear and continuous, and it generalizes to Tsallis entropy. Functoriality *is* the formal statement of the separation: the measure must respect maps on the reality side and not depend on how the organisation presents it. The payoff is that the separation isn't hygiene, it's what makes the measure canonical. Vigneaux's parallel result is that information cohomology is invariant under isomorphisms of generalized structures.

That passage is your separation, and it's the subject of Leinster's other book, *Higher Operads, Higher Categories*. There is a family of sheaves F_q such that Shannon entropy generates H¹(F₁) and Tsallis entropy generates H¹(F_q) for q ≠ 1. So your obstruction-classes-drive-evolution idea gets its first concrete instance: entropy is a degree-1 class, and the chain rule is the cocycle condition.

Note what this does to the profile parameter. Leinster's viewpoint parameter q, the Tsallis deformation, and the sheaf index F_q are the same q. Each viewpoint gets its own sheaf and its own entropy class. That is a precise version of "no privileged viewpoint, keep the coherent family."

On Connes–Rovelli: the relevant lesson is that a state on a von Neumann algebra generates its own one-parameter modular flow, canonical up to inner automorphisms via Connes' Radon–Nikodym *cocycle*. Dynamics emerges from the state rather than being imposed, which fits your explanation-not-prediction stance exactly. Cocycles appear on both sides here, and in the quantum setting relative entropy is genuinely built from the modular operator, so the link is real rather than merely verbal.

### Q

Is there a sense in which the structure that makes the adjunction between the world-categories and methodology-categories, and the structure that gives us optimality, are the same thing? And that they both are related to the entropy concepts discussed just above?

### A

Yes — and this is the tightest convergence in the whole conversation, because it's a theorem rather than an analogy.

**One structure: a profunctor.** Willerton's paper on the Legendre–Fenchel transform makes the pattern explicit. Start from a profunctor, i.e. a "matrix" of pairings between two sides. From it you construct an enriched adjunction between the presheaf categories, and then the nucleus is extracted as the parts on which the adjunction restricts to an equivalence. The construction is Pavlovic's nucleus of a profunctor.

That answers your question directly. The adjunction and the optimality locus are two readings of the same data:

- **The adjunction** is the profunctor's Isbell conjugation.
- **Optimality** is the nucleus, the region where unit and counit become isomorphisms.

And the enrichment decides which classical theory you get. With truth values, the adjunction is the Galois connection of a relation and the nucleus is the Galois correspondence. With the extended reals, the adjunction is the Legendre–Fenchel transform and the nucleus is Legendre–Fenchel duality (Toland–Singer), with convex functions as the fixed points.

So for our framework: fix a fit pairing between worlds and methods, valued in costs, and you get the method↔world adjunction, the asymmetric enriched metric I described earlier, and the well-adjusted region, all from one object. You don't build them separately.

**Where entropy enters, precisely.** In the real-valued case the adjunction is Legendre duality, and the quantity measuring failure to be in the nucleus is the Fenchel–Young gap: f(x) + f*(p) − ⟨x,p⟩, which is nonnegative and vanishes exactly at the optimum. That gap is the Bregman divergence, and when the convex generator is negative Shannon entropy, the Bregman divergence *is* relative entropy.

So the counit gap — what I earlier called the anthropic gap, how much of a world a method fails to recover — is a divergence, and in the entropic case it is literally relative entropy. That connects back to the Baez–Fritz–Leinster characterization of information loss from last time: the same quantity, now derived as the slack in an adjunction rather than posited.

Entropy and free energy are themselves a Legendre pair, so the Gibbs variational principle (equilibrium maximizes entropy under constraints, equivalently minimizes free energy) is exactly "being in the nucleus." That is also why the Connes–Rovelli side fits: the KMS equilibrium condition is this same optimum, and the modular flow and the optimality both come from the single object, the state. Same pattern as the profunctor giving both adjunction and nucleus.

**And magnitude closes the loop.** Leinster and Meckes showed maximum diversity is closely tied to magnitude, so the invariant I described as "the number extracted from an enriched category" is itself the *optimal value* of a diversity optimization. Magnitude is not an invariant that happens to be computable; it is what the optimization returns.

**The honest caveats.** Willerton's theorem is about enriched profunctors between concrete things; applying it needs an actual fit pairing between worlds and methods, valued in some quantale. And the Legendre story is genuinely tight only in the convex case — the nucleus is the convex part precisely because convexity is what makes biconjugation an involution. Non-convexity is exactly where duality gaps appear, which may be a feature: duality gaps would be your obstruction classes in this setting.

So the answer to all three questions is yes, with one qualification. The adjunction and optimality are the same structure viewed twice, entropy is the divergence measuring departure from the nucleus, and what makes it all one object is the profunctor. What's missing is not the mathematics but the pairing itself — someone has to say what the fit matrix between worlds and methods actually is.
