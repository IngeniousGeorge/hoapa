Lawvere's fixed-point theorem in a cartesian-closed category: if an object weakly
parametrises all maps into an object B, then every endomap of B has a fixed point;
contrapositively, a fixed-point-free operation on B (such as negation) makes such
total self-parametrisation impossible. It is the categorical generalisation of Gödel
incompleteness and Tarski undefinability. In the framework it is the "anti-sovereignty"
result: no sufficiently expressive, self-editing community can hold a complete
internal account of its own verification, so the system must stay open, stratified,
and self-revising. It is also the precise content of the single asymmetry on which
the conjecture's "if" turns — a category rich in fixed points versus one poor in them —
which is why the [cross-site-adjunction] has a direction rather than being a symmetric
equivalence.
