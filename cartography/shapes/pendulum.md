The pendulum is the general shape we use to read a domain of inquiry as a motion between two poles about an anchor, settling into a worked equilibrium — proposed as "the structure of the epistemology tag" for the whole framework. It declares four positions — [pendulum-anchor] (the immanent ground a domain departs from and must account for), [pendulum-left] (the demand for form, intelligibility, law), [pendulum-right] (the demand for novelty, genesis, problem), [pendulum-equilibrium] (the cared-for working balance a practice returns to) — and six invariant relations among them: [pendulum-formalization], [pendulum-problematization], [pendulum-poles-polemic], [pendulum-verification], [pendulum-stabilization], [pendulum-recontextualization]. As a template the shape is a pure set of these ten members; the directed skeleton lives in each operator's `from`/`to`. The load-bearing structural insight is that the equilibrium is *orthogonal* to the pole-axis — not a midpoint between left and right but a distinct working plane where the poles' polemic is metabolised into a practice, whose closing move ([pendulum-recontextualization]) hands a fresh anchor to the next swing, making the shape recursive rather than terminal. Two full instances draw it so far — [philosophy] and [mathematics], each `instantiates → pendulum` — while logic (its equilibrium seeded as [workilibrium-logic]) and science (the provisional [science-pendulum]) are only seeded, not yet built out into positions and edges.
