A dangling edge marking the node that has to exist before no-leak can be finished, and the reason its first rung must not be oversold. Blindness establishes that one observer — the acceptance check — cannot see participant labels. Leaking, though, is something an observer notices, so the general claim needs a class of trusted observations to quantify over: what the protocol lets anyone read off a record, beyond the verdict. Until that class is defined, there is not even a falsifying example available, since the checker was built with no identity-sensitive clause and so has nothing to catch. This is why the session's concrete checks are named degeneracy controls rather than leak tests, and why a runtime is not licensed by blindness to derive participant labels from real identities: the checker would not care, and everyone else could read them straight off.
