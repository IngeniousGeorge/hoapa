## Previous version of context, for reference:

### The pitch

Start with how one can combine notions in the philosophy of mathematics with ideas from the engineering of networks, to think an extension of the scientific method to the collective which incorporates participative democracy, or more generally a theory of work*. And recognize both what it can do, and its place in Nature.

\* For logicians, we can add: constrained by *permacultural* logic.

### The context

I started my journey into epistemology as the concerned citizen I've always been, but by chance, when I stumbled upon an interesting question, or rather an exercise: what is the most universal and balanced communication system possible? (And how to make this question rigorous.)

I call this system, and the whole project of understanding it in depth, Hoapa.

In this open letter addressed to Fernando Zalamea, I will present the point of view on the construction emanating from those specifications which best exhibits how important it could be, both as a *product* of science, and to *itself*, the scientific activity.

While my communication around my work has focused on the direct action prescribed by the latter, in this text I will embrace my role in the development of the former. Essentially, I'm now ready for the "big book" and 10 years of solitary work to which Zalamea urged me to commit.

The turning point for me has been the comprehension of Albert Lautman, after many perplexing attempts. In particular the movement he sees between dialectical or metaphysical problems (which can justify the construction), and solutions to those found in effective mathematical or physical theories (which resemble the construction).

The first part of the letter thus considers Hoapa through a series of dualities: concrete & abstract, synthetic & analytic, individuality & variation, in a context regulated by a tension between structure and genesis.

In the second part we broaden the epistemological scope, and revisit those concepts through the different authors who also gave them life in their work: Alexander Grothendieck, Bill Lawvere, Gilbert Simondon, Stuart Kauffman, Gilles Deleuze, Charles S. Peirce. Using those I will reconstruct the state the project is currently in: a conjectural adjunction between Hoapa as a process and some category representing "nice mathematics".

Third I will translate the design of the construction in the language of the Helsinki Logic Group, where I will present my work in the fall of 2026. This adaptation into model and set theoretic logic should demonstrate that our seemingly esoteric considerations do produce interesting and useful structures for the working mathematician.

I will also try and convince this audience that the logic they know and love is implicitly *ecologist*, that its structural laws participate in a universe designed for sustainability and solidarity. 

The name, Hoapa, comes from one of the many San languages and means "everywhere", as an homage to this culture, now largely extinct, who are the proud descendants of the first implementers of absolutely universal and balanced communication systems, masters of sustainability and solidarity. 

Hoapa is too humble and respectful of difference to be a theory of everything, but I believe its network shape and resilience allows it to be a theory of everywhere, true in every context.

## Ideas for:

### - Opening remarks

In the 19th century mathematicians were doing analysis, or arithmetic, or geometry, etc. In the 20th century they started doing algebraic geometry, differential geometry, etc. We could say that there are domains of science that are mostly left unexplored (with the notable exception of mathematical-physics, in each possible pair the reductionist agenda wants to debase the second element and picture it as "just the first element"; for each example we put in parenthesis the exceptions we're most interested in): philosophical mathematics (championed by Zalamea, contra analytic philosophy of mathematics à la Russell/Wittgenstein), physical-biology (Kauffman, EES), biological-psychology (attachment theory), organizational-social-science (Simondon, who says the psychological and social co-evolve, so the adequate first partner of the social is not the psychological but a more general "theory of organizations"). Hoapa's implementation lives in the last item in this list (and in the case of science is aimed at replacing the ad hoc system of peer reviews, mudded by its ties with the political history of "civilization"). But its theory is really an invariant in the general domain formed by all those mixtes/bridges.

### - Epistemological landscape

Besides making Kauffman the entry point into the point of view of Hoapa as a "theory of work", made out of constraints specified by separations of concerns, themselves guided by the dialectics, and embedded in the "primary" concrete/abstract tension, satisfying the closure conditions of Kauffman. The newest addition to our series of anchors is Peirce (a repositioning rather), which we can now say is the epistemological anchor for this theory of work viewpoint.

### - The argument

Abstract work can't reward its concrete substrate, the relation between abstract work and concrete substrate (where what 'abstract' and 'concrete' is is relative, as in some purely abstract things like mathematical objects can be considered concrete in relation to other mathematical objects which are even more abstract, as in Lautman) must be dedicated to a) mapping the context, in "intelligent" systems ([concrete <-> abstract] <-> world/context), b) the design (c -> a), and c) application (a -> c) of algorithms. The decomposition of abstract work in intelligent systems between a class of "synthetic (in the sense of Lautman) agents (as in the actor model)" and "analytic (Lautman) documented concepts (diagrammatic, many possible sources are relevant here—the analytic side is well developed in current science)", if we think of it as an object in a category of possible organizations of abstract work, would be a special object of that category (Hoapa's view of optimality).

### - Theory of everywhere

See relevant section in com*/presentation*.pdf. To which I would like to add the idea that it is a generalization of the Yoneda lemma to "worlds"/contexts, which can each be "reconstructed" from their web of relations to their neighboring worlds/contexts. And how this can be made precise following the concept of work as in 3. & 4., and mixtes as in the opening remarks and Zalamea's Lautman.
